## Finding ledger

| Severity | Finding | Closure |
|---|---|---|
| major | Domain names could become stereotypes about how work is organized | inspect actual authority and evidence system; guides explicitly reject assumptions |
| major | One senior person or act could create the appearance of several independent advocacy pathways | assess distinct functions, disclose overlap, and do not force a profile |
| major | Protected service or customer records could become employee evidence | use minimum authorized descriptions and preserve qualified record systems |
| major | Domain activity metrics could become proxy performance or advocacy scores | explicit cross-domain and local anti-proxy rules |
| major | US primary sources could be presented as universal legal rules | sources are boundary examples, jurisdiction-specific, and not legal interpretation |
| minor | Care, education, and public service may be too broad for one guide | defer split decision until practitioners show materially different LIFT actions |

## Disposition

No critical or major actionable synthetic design finding remains.

The domain layer is accepted as a role-reviewed draft. Independent practitioners
from the named settings should test:

1. whether the guide describes their actual authority structure;
2. whether ordinary evidence can be reused without privacy or workload harm;
3. whether the bounded action is feasible for their schedule and leverage;
4. whether the guide preserves qualified reporting and professional duties;
5. whether any combined guide should split or any repeated guide should retire.
