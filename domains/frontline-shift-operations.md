# LIFT in Frontline, Shift, and Operations Work

Use this guide for site, field, logistics, manufacturing, retail, hospitality,
service, maintenance, or other schedule-bound operational work.

## Conditions to inspect

Do not assume that the person who assigns a shift also controls development,
resources, discipline, safety response, or broader advocacy.

Inspect:

- shift, site, region, vendor, and functional authority;
- who sees handoffs, quality, maintenance, prevention, and recovery work;
- whether night, weekend, remote-site, and mobile workers reach the same forums;
- time, language, accessibility, device, and connectivity constraints;
- union, safety, incident, and protected reporting routes.

## Translate the evidence path

| Channel | Ordinary evidence | Common distortion |
|---|---|---|
| work | handoffs, quality checks, service records, maintenance decisions, incident prevention, customer outcomes | visible crises outweigh routine prevention |
| context | shift notes, site constraints, staffing, equipment, demand, weather, inherited backlog | central reviewers see output without local conditions |
| credit | named decisions, improvements, recoveries, training, coordination | presenter or day shift receives the whole story |
| opportunity | qualification, cross-training, acting assignments, improvement work, schedule access | availability is mistaken for readiness or commitment |

Reuse authorized operational records where possible. Do not create a second
incident, safety, customer, or personnel database.

## Interpret M/S/E

- **M** is the actual direct manager with relevant responsibility and an
  opportunity to represent the work. A scheduler or shift lead is not M unless
  they hold that management responsibility.
- **S** is the next accountable operational or functional leader, not merely a
  scheduler or senior person on site.
- **E** is an enterprise, regional, owner, or senior sponsor when that layer is
  relevant to the work.

Record `unknown` when authority is split or the pathway cannot be observed.

## Synthetic example

A night-shift technician prevents repeated downtime through routine
maintenance. The site review highlights the day-shift recovery lead because
that person presents the weekly summary.

A bounded response is to preserve the maintenance decision in the existing
reliability record, have the accountable manager carry accurate shared credit,
and include the relevant owner when future prevention decisions are discussed.

Do not invent avoided-loss figures or require the technician to produce weekly
self-marketing reports. Any necessary review or correction should have paid
time, an accessible format, and an authorized device or channel.

## Stop or route elsewhere

Use the qualified route for safety hazards, retaliation, wage or scheduling
rights, discrimination, union matters, serious misconduct, or regulated
incidents. LIFT may examine whether ordinary work evidence travels fairly; it
must not replace those processes.
