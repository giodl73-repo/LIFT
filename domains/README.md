# LIFT Domain Field Guides

LIFT's modules remain authoritative. These guides translate the framework into
different work settings without changing M/S/E, adding domain scores, or
claiming that one operating model fits every workplace.

## Choose a field guide

| Work setting | Guide |
|---|---|
| shift, site, field, logistics, manufacturing, or service operations | [Frontline, Shift, and Operations](frontline-shift-operations.md) |
| software, hardware, data, research, design, or product development | [Engineering and Product](engineering-product.md) |
| sales, account management, support, success, or customer operations | [Sales and Customer Service](sales-customer-service.md) |
| healthcare, education, government, or other public-service work | [Care, Education, and Public Service](care-education-public-service.md) |
| nonprofit, community, board-governed, or volunteer work | [Nonprofit and Volunteer Organizations](nonprofit-volunteer.md) |
| consulting, agency, contract, freelance, or contingent work | [Consulting, Contract, and Contingent Work](consulting-contract-contingent.md) |
| founder-led, early-stage, family, or small organizations | [Startups and Small Businesses](startups-small-business.md) |

Choose the guide that best matches the authority and evidence system, not only
the industry name. A hospital software team may need both the engineering guide
and the care/privacy boundaries. A nonprofit shop may need both the operations
and volunteer guides.

This first set is representative, not exhaustive. Add another guide only when
the work setting changes evidence, authority, feasible action, or a safety
boundary in a way these guides do not cover.

## Domain-guide contract

Every field guide should:

- preserve the definitions and 1-10 rubric in Module 00;
- identify who actually holds direct, next-level, and enterprise authority;
- translate ordinary work evidence without demanding a new shadow record;
- distinguish work evidence from advocacy evidence;
- include less visible, maintenance, coordination, assurance, and recovery work;
- account for schedule, location, language, accessibility, and employment
  structure;
- name records that must not be copied into LIFT;
- preserve formal, protected, contractual, professional, and safety routes;
- use synthetic examples and retain `unknown`;
- stop when LIFT is not the qualified process.

Do not fill every field merely because it exists.

## Cross-domain interpretation rules

### Authority beats title

A senior title does not automatically make someone S or E. Record whether the
person has the relevant authority, context, and opportunity to advocate.

When one person holds several layers of authority, disclose the overlap. Do not
present one relationship as several independent advocacy pathways.

### Proximity is not advocacy

Working near a founder, clinician, principal, customer, board member, or
executive does not establish support. Access can be frequent and still
transactional, constrained, or unequally distributed.

### Activity is not contribution

Do not infer contribution or advocacy from:

- message, meeting, ticket, call, commit, or shift counts;
- office, site, travel, or camera presence;
- sales volume without territory and shared-work context;
- incident volume without assignment and reporting context;
- customer, patient, student, donor, or beneficiary records;
- network centrality or informal access.

### Qualified systems retain authority

LIFT does not replace:

- safety reporting and incident response;
- clinical, educational, professional, or public duties;
- legal, regulatory, contractual, labor, or union processes;
- privacy, records-management, or information-security rules;
- formal investigations or employment decisions.

settings. A guide should be simplified, split, or retired if it merely repeats
office-work assumptions, adds documentation burden, or obscures real authority.
