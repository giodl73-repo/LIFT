# Synthetic Domain Field-Guide Scenarios

These invented scenarios test whether the domain guides change a concrete
evidence, authority, action, or safety decision without changing LIFT's core
model.

For each scenario:

1. identify actual M/S/E authority or decline to force a profile;
2. select ordinary authorized evidence;
3. name evidence that must not be reused;
4. identify one pathway distortion;
5. choose one feasible bounded action;
6. preserve a qualified or protected route;
7. record what remains `unknown`.

## Scenario A: night-shift prevention

A warehouse technician on the night shift identifies a recurring scanner
failure and changes a maintenance handoff.

- the shift lead assigns work but does not manage the technician;
- the site manager reviews only the day-shift summary;
- reliability records show the handoff decision;
- the technician has no company device outside the shift;
- a supervisor asks for weekly personal-phone photos and narratives;
- no evidence shows what the regional leader knows.

Stress checks:

- do not treat the shift lead as M without management responsibility;
- do not require unpaid evidence work or personal-device use;
- preserve safety and retaliation routes.

## Scenario B: invisible technical assurance

A security reviewer identifies a release risk and works with an engineer to
change the design.

- the product lead presents the successful launch;
- the reviewer has few commits and no roadmap presentation;
- review and decision records show the material change;
- a dotted-line technical lead directs review standards but is not the
  reviewer's manager;
- no evidence shows senior sponsorship.

Stress checks:

- do not use commits, messages, or meeting counts as contribution;
- keep technical authority separate from M/S/E;
- retain E as `unknown`.

## Scenario C: inherited sales conditions

An account manager inherits a territory with delayed renewals.

- a support specialist identifies a recurring product defect;
- a solution consultant designs the workaround;
- the account manager presents the renewal plan;
- call recordings contain confidential customer details;
- the manager compares revenue without territory context;
- the next-level leader has not reviewed the shared contribution.

Stress checks:

- distinguish outcome metrics from advocacy evidence;
- do not copy customer recordings into LIFT;
- preserve shared contribution without percentage allocation.

## Scenario D: professional duty and private records

A public-service professional identifies a service handoff that may create
harm.

- a qualified reporting route exists;
- the direct manager prefers to wait for a routine review;
- protected service-recipient records contain the detailed evidence;
- an authorized deidentified operational record can describe the handoff;
- no evidence shows next-level or enterprise advocacy.

Stress checks:

- the professional duty does not wait for a better score;
- do not create a parallel protected case file;
- route the concern before using LIFT for pathway reflection.

## Scenario E: mission and unpaid labor

A volunteer coordinator keeps a community program running after two staff
departures.

- the executive publicly receives credit;
- the board sees only grant outputs;
- volunteer schedules and program records show the coordination work;
- the coordinator is pressured to accept more unpaid responsibility;
- worker status and expense questions are disputed.

Stress checks:

- recognition is not workload repair;
- do not infer legal worker status;
- make refusal and qualified governance or classification routes visible.

## Scenario F: two organizations

An agency contractor resolves a client dependency.

- the client program lead praises the work;
- the agency account lead presents the result internally;
- the contractor's actual manager has not reviewed the delivery record;
- the client contract restricts what can be shared;
- renewal and payment questions are active.

Stress checks:

- do not combine client and employer relationships into one M/S/E profile;
- use only authorized evidence;
- route contract and payment questions outside LIFT.

## Scenario G: overlapping founder authority

In a small company, the founder is both direct manager and enterprise owner.

- the founder accurately credits an operations lead in one customer meeting;
- there is no distinct next management layer;
- no other senior sponsor exists;
- operational records show the lead stabilized delivery;
- after-hours access to the founder is uneven across the team.

Stress checks:

- disclose overlapping M and E authority;
- do not present one founder relationship as two independent paths;
- do not invent S;
- do not use after-hours proximity as advocacy evidence.

## Passing result

A guide passes a scenario when it produces a safe, feasible pathway action and
rejects the misuse trap without adding a domain score, new profile identity, or
shadow record.

A guide should be revised, split, or retired when it cannot do so.
