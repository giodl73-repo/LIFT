# Module 01 Claim Ledger

| Claim | Label | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| People observe only part of organizational advocacy | practitioner hypothesis | retain with explicit uncertainty |
| Self-assessment can diverge from external evidence | externally supported in adjacent professional domains | retain without claiming an M/S/E effect size |
| Feedback seeking can support workplace learning | externally supported | retain with context and safety limits |
| Leadership affects whether people seek and use feedback | externally supported | retain |
| Sponsorship differs from mentorship through active advocacy and organizational capital | externally supported concept | retain |
| Asking for a specific recent advocacy example is more useful than asking for reassurance | practice and practitioner hypothesis | retain; independent LIFT testing needed |
| Direct, reported, ambient, and inferred evidence should be separated | LIFT practice | retain |
| Triangulation increases confidence | practice with adjacent research support | retain without claiming certainty |
| People overestimate M by a fixed amount | synthetic/private claim | remove |
| People should automatically increase E estimates | synthetic/private claim | remove |
| The sponsor question produces a quantified bias correction | synthetic/private claim | remove |
| Evasion proves retention suppression or hidden opposition | unsupported and harmful inference | remove |
| One answer determines the correct score | unsupported | reject |

## Open evidence needs

- independent practitioners using the evidence table and sponsor question;
- examples from flat, matrixed, frontline, nonprofit, government, academic, and
  cooperative organizations;
- research on feedback seeking under power asymmetry;
- evidence about whether repeated M/S/E reflection increases agency or anxiety;
- counterexamples where specific advocacy evidence still failed to produce
  expected opportunity or support.
