# Module 01: Honest Assessment

Status: draft

## Purpose

An M/S/E score is a hypothesis built from incomplete evidence. This module
helps practitioners improve the evidence record before changing the number.

The goal is not perfect access to private conversations. The goal is a score or
range that clearly distinguishes:

- what was observed;
- what was reported by someone else;
- what was inferred;
- what remains unknown.

## The observation gap

Advocacy often occurs when the person being discussed is absent. That creates a
real measurement problem:

```text
visible relationship behavior
is not the same as
advocacy performed through organizational channels
```

A supportive 1:1, positive feedback, a meeting invitation, or public praise may
matter. None proves what happened in another conversation.

This does not mean hidden advocacy is absent. It means the evidence is
incomplete.

## Four evidence classes

| Class | Meaning | Typical confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Direct act | You observed a decision, introduction, resource, opportunity, protection, or attribution connected to advocacy | strongest |
| Reported act | Someone described a specific advocacy act that occurred elsewhere | useful, but source-dependent |
| Ambient signal | Warmth, praise, visibility, meeting access, reputation, or generic support | contextual, not sufficient alone |
| Inference | A conclusion about private intent without an observable or reported act | do not score from this alone |

Direct does not always mean face-to-face. A written decision, invitation,
explicit attribution, or documented scope change can be a direct act.

Reported evidence should record who supplied the account and how close that
person was to the event. Do not secretly investigate private conversations.

## Common assessment errors

### Warmth substitution

Replacing:

> What advocacy act occurred?

with:

> Does this person like and encourage me?

Warmth can coexist with sponsorship, mentorship, neutrality, weak authority, or
conflicting incentives.

### Visibility substitution

Being known is not the same as receiving informed support. A leader may
recognize a name without understanding the work, or understand the work without
being positioned to sponsor it.

### Recency anchoring

One compliment or one difficult conversation should not reset a score built
from a longer pattern. Record the event and ask whether it changes the pattern.

### Outcome hindsight

A favorable outcome does not prove strong advocacy, and an unfavorable outcome
does not prove its absence. Budgets, role availability, timing, competition,
performance evidence, and organizational change also matter.

### Access pessimism and optimism

Do not assume that senior advocacy is absent because you cannot observe it. Do
not assume that it exists because your work is visible.

Use `unknown`.

### Precision forcing

If evidence supports `5–6`, do not choose 6 because a form expects one number.
If evidence supports no responsible range, record `unknown`.

## Assessment process

### 1. Set the frame

Record:

```text
Dimension:
Assessment date:
Evidence window:
Question being assessed:
```

Examples of a question:

- Does my manager advocate for the scope I currently own?
- Does the next organizational level understand my contribution independently?
- Is there informed senior sponsorship for this cross-organization initiative?

Do not mix several roles, time periods, or goals into one score.

### 2. Inventory evidence before scoring

Use:

| Evidence | Class | Date | Supports | Weakens | Source distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | direct / reported / ambient / inference | | M / S / E | M / S / E | direct / one step / multiple steps |

List counterevidence with the same care as favorable evidence.

### 3. Separate mentorship, visibility, and sponsorship

| Relationship behavior | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|
| Advice, coaching, listening, and feedback | mentorship or management support |
| Invitations, presentations, and name recognition | visibility or access |
| Accurate credit, nomination, opportunity creation, protection of scope, and advocacy under constraint | sponsorship evidence |

These can overlap. LIFT does not treat mentorship as lesser or defective.
Mentorship can be valuable even when sponsorship is not present or appropriate.

### 4. Ask for evidence safely

The core manager question is:

> Can you share a recent example of how you represented my work, growth, or
> scope in a conversation I was not part of?

Possible follow-ups:

- What part of my contribution did you emphasize?
- What evidence would make your advocacy more specific?
- Where is my work currently understood accurately, and where is it not?
- What should I demonstrate before you would recommend broader scope?

For S:

> What observable evidence suggests the next organizational level understands
> my contribution independently?

For E:

> What decision, resource, scope, or protection demonstrates informed senior
> sponsorship?

No one is required to ask these questions directly. When the relationship is
unsafe, punitive, discriminatory, or highly unstable, use safer channels:

- clarify expectations through an ordinary development conversation;
- review documented decisions and opportunities;
- seek feedback from a trusted advisor who already has legitimate context;
- use an established employee-support or reporting process where appropriate;
- leave the score unknown.

Do not recruit colleagues to monitor private conversations.

### 5. Interpret without mind-reading

| Response | Responsible interpretation |
|---|---|
| Specific recent example with a named action or outcome | one useful evidence item |
| General reassurance without an example | support may exist, but advocacy evidence remains weak or unknown |
| No example because the opportunity has not arisen | advocacy is untested, not necessarily absent |
| Critical feedback or stated readiness gap | direct evidence about current support conditions |
| Defensive, uncomfortable, or evasive response | evidence about conversation safety or clarity, not proof of hidden intent |

One answer should rarely determine an entire score.

### 6. Triangulate

When safe and legitimate, compare:

- the person's account;
- documented decisions or opportunities;
- what another informed participant can responsibly confirm;
- whether credit and context remain accurate across organizational levels;
- counterexamples where expected advocacy did not occur.

Triangulation increases confidence. It does not create certainty about private
beliefs.

### 7. Assign a range and confidence

Record:

```text
Provisional score or range:
Confidence: low / medium / high
Strongest supporting evidence:
Strongest counterevidence:
Unknowns:
What would change the range:
```

Suggested confidence interpretation:

| Confidence | Evidence pattern |
|---|---|
| low | mostly ambient, old, indirect, contradictory, or incomplete |
| medium | at least one specific direct or reported act with meaningful unknowns |
| high | several recent, specific acts with consistent counterevidence review and responsible corroboration |

Confidence describes the evidence behind the score. It is not a fourth advocacy
dimension and should not be multiplied into M, S, or E.

### 8. Reassess only when evidence changes

Do not rescore after every interaction. Reassess after:

- a material decision or opportunity;
- a development or scope conversation;
- a manager or organizational change;
- evidence that contradicts the current range;
- enough time for the prior evidence to become stale.

The appropriate cadence depends on the work. Constant scoring can increase
anxiety and encourage political performance instead of better management.

## Dimension-specific cautions

### M

M is the most conversationally accessible dimension, but the relationship can
make warmth substitution especially tempting. Ask for examples without
demanding disclosure of confidential discussions.

### S

Next-level visibility is often mistaken for advocacy. A presentation or
positive comment is useful ambient evidence; it is not automatically informed
support.

### E

E is frequently the least observable dimension. `unknown` is often more honest
than either pessimistic or optimistic correction.

Senior sponsorship may attach to a problem, role, or initiative rather than to
one individual's career. Record the scope being assessed.

## Actions after assessment

A score should lead to one of four actions:

1. **Clarify evidence:** ask what is known, missing, or disputed.
2. **Improve the work or its explanation:** make outcomes and contribution
   easier to understand accurately.
3. **Improve the pathway:** strengthen legitimate communication, attribution,
   introductions, and decision context.
4. **Recognize a structural constraint:** change expectations, seek support,
   revise the scope, or consider a different environment.

Do not optimize the number through performative visibility, favoritism,
information manipulation, or coercive networking.

## What this module does not claim

- People systematically mis-score M or E by a universal numerical amount.
- A fixed arithmetic correction improves an M/S/E score.
- A vague or evasive answer proves hidden opposition.
- Unseen senior sponsorship should be assumed upward or downward.
- Feedback seeking is safe in every workplace.
- One conversation reveals a person's private intent.
- A more accurate score guarantees a better career outcome.

## External evidence boundary

Public research supports adjacent ideas: professional self-assessment can be
imperfect, feedback seeking can support workplace learning, leadership shapes
feedback-seeking conditions, and sponsorship differs from mentorship through
active use of organizational influence.

Those findings do not validate LIFT's M/S/E scale or any universal correction
formula. See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).
