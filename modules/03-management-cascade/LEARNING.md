# Module 03 Learning

## Source lesson

The private source identified a valuable management mechanism: a manager's own
context, credibility, and relationships affect which evidence and
opportunities can move into and out of a team.

It overstated that mechanism through generated correlations, hard score
ceilings, deterministic pattern inheritance, and precise claims about
organizational scale and distributed work.

## Changes in the public rewrite

- the cascade became five inspectable channels rather than a score multiplier;
- manager influence became amplification or attenuation rather than destiny;
- absence, delay, distortion, concentration, and dependency became shared
  pathway failure modes;
- a report's S and E must be assessed from direct evidence, not inferred from
  the manager;
- administrative exposure was separated from awareness, advocacy, and
  sponsorship;
- manager departure became a continuity review rather than a numerical reset;
- direct and redundant routes replaced permanent manager brokerage;
- network size and meeting count were rejected as performance measures;
- boundary-spanning costs and overload became explicit;
- structural and equity responsibility moved beyond the individual manager;
- fixed remote-work and organizational-size formulas were removed.

## Open learning questions

- Are the five channels distinct enough for practitioners to map?
- Does the shared failure vocabulary reveal actionable problems?
- When is redundancy worth its coordination cost?
- Can the pathway map remain lightweight?
- How should legitimate confidentiality limit context sharing?
- When does direct access become bypass or overload?
- Can organizations review unequal pathways without creating surveillance?
- Does the module reduce blame while preserving manager accountability?
