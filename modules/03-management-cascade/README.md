# Module 03: The Management Cascade

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module examines how a manager's organizational pathways affect a team's
access to context, evidence, resources, opportunity, and relationships.

A management cascade is:

> the movement of organizational inputs into a team and team evidence out to
> the people who can understand, support, resource, or act on it.

"Cascade" does not mean that organizations should operate only from the top
down. It names a recurring dependency: managers often sit between a team and
other parts of the organization.

The manager can strengthen or weaken a pathway. The manager is not a hard
ceiling and does not determine another person's future.

## The five channels

```text
                    context
                       |
relationships <- manager and team -> evidence
                       |
             resources and opportunity
```

The channels interact, but they should be inspected separately.

### 1. Context

Context moves into the team:

- organizational priorities;
- customer or stakeholder needs;
- constraints and tradeoffs;
- decision rationale;
- changes in risk, funding, ownership, or timing.

A manager contributes by acquiring, translating, and testing context.

Common failures:

- **absence:** relevant context never arrives;
- **delay:** the team learns after a decision is effectively fixed;
- **distortion:** uncertainty becomes certainty or politics becomes strategy;
- **concentration:** only a favored few receive the context;
- **dependency:** the manager is the only person allowed to interpret it.

Translation should simplify without manufacturing confidence or hiding
disagreement.

### 2. Evidence

Evidence moves out of the team:

- contribution and learning;
- delivery and operational results;
- customer or stakeholder outcomes;
- risks, misses, and constraints;
- capability and readiness for broader scope.

A manager contributes by preserving accuracy, connecting evidence to relevant
decisions, and ensuring it reaches an appropriate audience.

This channel builds on [Advocacy in Action](../02-advocacy-in-action/README.md).
The cascade question is not only "Did the manager advocate?" but "Did the
evidence arrive accurately, in time, through a resilient route?"

### 3. Resources

Resources move into and across the team:

- people and time;
- budget;
- tools and infrastructure;
- information;
- specialist support;
- decision authority.

A manager may acquire resources, negotiate tradeoffs, or explain why a request
was declined.

Resource failure should not automatically become a judgment about an
individual's worth or the manager's effort. Some constraints are outside the
manager's control. The diagnostic question is whether the constraint, decision,
and available alternatives are visible.

### 4. Opportunity

Opportunity moves toward people:

- meaningful assignments;
- decision participation;
- presentations and forums;
- introductions;
- expanded ownership;
- development and sponsorship.

A manager contributes by making selection criteria visible, allocating access
fairly, and creating support for success.

An opportunity-rich manager can still create a weak cascade by concentrating
access among familiar or favored people.

### 5. Relationships

Relationships can move from manager-owned to team-accessible:

- working connections across teams;
- informed next-level leaders;
- senior sponsors;
- professional communities;
- customer or stakeholder relationships;
- peers who can verify contribution.

A manager contributes by creating legitimate direct relationships rather than
remaining the permanent broker for every interaction.

Direct access is not unrestricted bypass. It should have a work purpose,
appropriate consent, clear decision rights, and respect for organizational
coordination.

## Five pathway failures

Use the same failure vocabulary across all channels.

| Failure | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| Absence | Does a usable route exist? |
| Delay | Does the input or evidence arrive while action is still possible? |
| Distortion | Does meaning remain accurate as it travels? |
| Concentration | Who repeatedly receives access, and who does not? |
| Dependency | What happens if the manager is unavailable, changes role, or leaves? |

These are pathway findings, not personality diagnoses.

## Amplification and attenuation

A manager amplifies a pathway when they:

- acquire useful context and share it in time;
- make team evidence legible without inflating it;
- connect people to resources and decision-makers;
- distribute opportunity with relevant criteria;
- create more informed and direct relationships;
- preserve continuity through records and shared ownership.

A manager attenuates a pathway when they:

- withhold or delay context;
- compress shared contribution into a hero story;
- relay only favorable evidence;
- hoard external relationships;
- allocate visibility repeatedly through familiarity;
- make themselves the only source, interpreter, or sponsor.

Amplification is not maximum volume. More meetings, mentions, introductions,
or contacts can create noise, overload, and coordination cost.

## The manager is not a ceiling

LIFT rejects formulas such as:

```text
your S cannot exceed your manager's S
your E cannot exceed your manager's E
your trajectory is inherited from your manager
```

A manager's weak pathway may make access harder. Other legitimate paths may
include:

- direct project relationships;
- cross-functional leaders;
- communities of practice;
- customer or stakeholder evidence;
- formal review and documentation;
- next-level conversations;
- another mentor or sponsor;
- a role, team, or organizational change.

These alternatives do not make the manager irrelevant. They prevent one
relationship from becoming a total explanation.

## Exposure is not advocacy

Organizational scope may create:

- attendance in senior forums;
- administrative visibility;
- budget or planning contact;
- a larger formal remit.

None of these proves that a senior person understands the work or will advocate
for a person or team.

Record the observed behavior:

```text
Exposure: attended a review where the team was discussed
Awareness: can accurately describe the team's contribution
Advocacy: supported a specific evidence-based claim
Sponsorship: used influence or accepted cost to create an opportunity
```

Do not convert team size, meeting count, title, or access into an automatic
M/S/E score.

## The pathway map

Use one record per meaningful channel.

```text
Channel:
Needed movement:
Current route:
Evidence that it arrived:
Failure mode, if any:
Who has and lacks access:
Fallback or alternate route:
Next bounded experiment:
Evidence date:
```

Example:

```text
Channel: context
Needed movement: understand why the service deadline changed
Current route: senior review -> manager -> weekly team meeting
Evidence that it arrived: team can explain the new customer constraint
Failure mode, if any: delay; implementation began before the explanation
Who has and lacks access: project leads heard first; operations heard later
Fallback or alternate route: shared decision note and operations representative
Next bounded experiment: publish decision note within one working day
Evidence date: 2026-08-07
```

The record maps movement. It does not score the manager's popularity, network,
or political skill.

## Build pathway resilience

Resilience means the team can continue to receive context and carry evidence
when one person is unavailable.

Useful practices:

1. document consequential decisions and evidence;
2. create direct working relationships where appropriate;
3. ensure more than one person understands critical external context;
4. separate relationship ownership from information ownership;
5. name shared contribution accurately;
6. include transition and continuity planning before a manager change;
7. maintain more than one informed advocacy path where practical.

Redundancy is not indiscriminate networking. Add routes where dependency has a
meaningful cost.

## Manager departure or change

A manager transition can weaken:

- current context;
- historical evidence;
- sponsorship commitments;
- resource negotiations;
- cross-team coordination.

Before or during a transition:

```text
Which decisions and rationale need a durable record?
Who can verify each person's contribution?
Which commitments were made, and by whom?
Which relationships should become direct or shared?
Which access depended only on the departing manager?
What must the incoming manager relearn rather than inherit as fact?
```

Do not assume every relationship, judgment, or sponsorship commitment transfers.
Do not assume all evidence resets to zero.

## Organizational responsibility

The cascade is not solely a manager self-improvement problem.

Senior leaders and organizations should:

- create forums where evidence can travel without personal patronage;
- make resource and opportunity criteria inspectable;
- prevent distributed workers from depending on proximity alone;
- review who receives consequential access;
- support managers doing boundary work;
- avoid overloading one manager as the only translator or broker;
- preserve continuity during reorganizations and departures;
- provide appeal or correction routes for material misrepresentation.

If the only route to context or opportunity is a manager's private network,
the organization owns part of the risk.

## Equity and power

Network access is not evenly distributed.

Review:

- who is introduced and who remains represented secondhand;
- whose work is easy to explain to powerful audiences;
- whose location, schedule, identity, role, or employment arrangement limits
  informal access;
- whether the same people repeatedly receive scarce forums;
- whether relationship-building expectations reward similarity and proximity;
- whether declining visibility is treated as lack of ambition.

The remedy is not to require everyone to perform more political labor. Redesign
the route where possible.

## M/S/E interpretation

Module 03 adds pathway context to an M/S/E evidence record.

```text
M evidence may show:
- whether the direct manager understands and represents the work;

S evidence may show:
- whether next-level awareness or advocacy exists through any legitimate route;

E evidence may show:
- whether senior awareness, advocacy, or sponsorship exists through any
  legitimate route.
```

Do not infer a report's S or E from the manager's score. Record evidence at the
relevant layer.

## What this module does not claim

- A manager's network determines a team member's outcome.
- A person's M/S/E profile is inherited from their manager.
- A report's score is capped one point below the manager's score.
- A manager with more contacts is necessarily more effective.
- Large organizational scope creates senior sponsorship.
- Distributed work carries a universal numerical penalty.
- Direct senior access is always desirable or safe.
- Every pathway needs redundancy.
- Boundary spanning has no cost.
- A manager can repair structural discrimination through advocacy alone.
