# Module 03 Public Sources

These sources support adjacent concepts. They do not validate the LIFT
five-channel map, manager-to-report score formulas, hard ceilings, or career
predictions.

## Team networks and leadership

- Balkundi, P., and Harrison, D. A. (2006), "Ties, Leaders, and Time in
  Teams: Strong Inference About Network Structure's Effects on Team Viability
  and Performance," *Academy of Management Journal*, 49(1), 49-68.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2006.20785500).

  This meta-analysis associates team and leader network structures with team
  viability and performance. It does not show that a manager's network
  determines an individual team member's career outcome.

## External activity and boundary management

- Ancona, D. G., and Caldwell, D. F. (1992), "Bridging the Boundary: External
  Activity and Performance in Organizational Teams," *Administrative Science
  Quarterly*, 37(4), 634-665.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2393475).

  The study distinguishes forms of external activity and their different
  relationships with team performance. LIFT uses it to avoid treating all
  external contact as equally useful.

- Marrone, J. A. (2010), "Team Boundary Spanning: A Multilevel Review of Past
  Research and Proposals for the Future," *Journal of Management*, 36(4),
  911-940.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206309353944).

  This review describes boundary spanning as a multilevel, context-dependent
  activity rather than an unlimited good.

- Leicht-Deobald, U., Backmann, J., de Vries, T. A., and colleagues (2025),
  "A Contingency Framework for the Performance Consequences of Team Boundary
  Management: A Meta-Analysis of 30 Years of Research," *Journal of
  Management*, 51(2), 704-747.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/01492063231206107).

  The meta-analysis finds generally positive but contingent performance
  relationships depending on the activity, target, and person carrying the
  boundary work. It does not support scoring contact volume or assuming that
  more boundary activity is always better.

- Lan, Y., Li, C., Wang, J., and Meng, X. (2022), "Benefits and Costs of
  Employee Boundary-Spanning Behavior: A Meta-Analytic Review," *Acta
  Psychologica Sinica*, 54(6), 665-683.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3724/SP.J.1041.2022.00665).

  This review reports both beneficial outcomes and costs such as role stress
  and exhaustion. LIFT uses it to keep workload and coordination costs visible.

## Sponsorship and unequal access

- Ayyala, M. S., et al. (2019), "Mentorship Is Not Enough: Exploring
  Sponsorship and Its Role in Career Advancement in Academic Medicine,"
  *Academic Medicine*, 94(1), 94-100.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/ACM.0000000000002398).

  The article distinguishes sponsorship from mentorship and discusses the use
  of influence to create visibility and opportunity. Its setting is academic
  medicine and should not be generalized without care.

- Schwartz, R., Williams, M., and Feldman, M. (2024), "Does Sponsorship
  Promote Equity in Career Advancement in Academic Medicine? A Scoping
  Review," *Journal of General Internal Medicine*, 39.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-023-08542-4).

  The review examines sponsorship and equity in a specific professional
  setting. LIFT uses it as evidence that access and distribution require
  explicit attention, not as proof of outcomes in every workplace.
