# Module 00: M/S/E Foundation

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module defines the smallest useful LIFT model: three organizational
advocacy layers and an evidence-linked 1–10 score for each.

## The three dimensions

### M: direct-manager advocacy

M asks whether your direct manager has enough specific evidence and conviction
to support your work, growth, scope, or decisions when you are not present.

Evidence may include:

- specific feedback tied to outcomes;
- resources or scope entrusted to you;
- accurate credit in conversations you did not attend;
- support during disagreement or uncertainty;
- concrete development investment.

M is not a rating of whether your manager is friendly or whether you enjoy the
relationship.

### S: next-level or skip advocacy

S asks whether the next organizational level has an independent, accurate view
of your contribution and can act on it when cross-team context, calibration,
resources, or opportunity require broader authority.

Evidence may include:

- the next-level leader can describe your contribution without a script;
- your work is connected to broader priorities accurately;
- decisions or opportunities reflect direct knowledge rather than generic
  reputation;
- your manager can relay evidence through a trusted channel.

S does not require personal closeness or constant exposure.

### E: executive or senior-sponsor advocacy

E asks whether a sufficiently senior leader understands and supports the scope,
problem, or organizational value connected to your work.

Evidence may include:

- senior leaders reference the work accurately;
- sponsorship creates or protects consequential scope;
- your contribution is connected to an organizational decision;
- senior context reaches you through a credible sponsorship pathway.

In smaller organizations, E may mean a founder, owner, board member, department
head, or another person with enterprise-level authority.

## The 1–10 evidence rubric

| Score | Evidence interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 1–2 | No reliable awareness, materially inaccurate understanding, or active negative evidence |
| 3–4 | Some awareness, but little evidence of informed support |
| 5–6 | Accurate awareness and conditional support; advocacy is inconsistent or untested |
| 7–8 | Recent, specific, repeated advocacy supported by observable acts |
| 9–10 | Sustained sponsorship remains visible when it carries cost, risk, disagreement, or scarce opportunity |

Scores are ordinal judgments. The difference between 7 and 8 is not a measured
physical unit, and two people's scores are not automatically comparable. Use a
range such as `5–6` when the evidence does not support a single value.

## Evidence before score

Record:

```text
Dimension:
Provisional score or range:
Observable evidence:
Counterevidence:
Evidence date:
Confidence: low / medium / high
What would change the score:
```

If evidence is missing, record `unknown`. Do not turn uncertainty into a
midpoint score.

## The LIFT profile

Write the three scores as:

```text
M·S·E
```

Example:

```text
7·5–6·unknown
```

The lowest known dimension identifies the advocacy path that currently deserves
the most inquiry. It does not prove that the path caused a promotion, retention,
performance, hiring, or compensation outcome.

Do not sum or average M, S, and E into one health score. A high value cannot
cancel a weak or unknown evidence path.

## Diagnostic questions

For M:

> What specific action shows that my manager represented my work accurately
> when I was not present?

For S:

> What observable action indicates that the next organizational level has an
> independent and accurate view of my contribution?

For E:

> Which senior decision, resource, scope, or protection demonstrates informed
> sponsorship rather than visibility alone?

## Responsible interpretation

A low score can mean many different things:

- evidence has not had time to form;
- the work is not reaching the relevant layer;
- the manager or organizational channel is weak;
- the scope does not require that layer yet;
- access is structurally unequal;
- the score is wrong;
- performance or role expectations are unclear;
- organizational conditions make sponsorship scarce.

The next action is investigation, not self-blame or political theater.

## Ethical use

LIFT is primarily a self-reflection and development-conversation framework.
Do not secretly score reports, candidates, peers, or managers and then use the
result as a performance, promotion, hiring, succession, or termination record.

When another person is part of a score:

- disclose the purpose when it is safe and appropriate;
- separate observed behavior from inferred intent;
- invite correction and counterevidence;
- treat low access as a system condition rather than a personal deficiency;
- do not publish the score without permission.

## What this module does not claim

- M/S/E determines promotion.
- Universal promotion thresholds exist.
- Advocacy is more important than contribution.
- A person can accurately infer private opinions from indirect signals.
- Everyone has equal access to managers, next-level leaders, or executives.
- Raising a score is always the right response.
- Adding or averaging the three scores creates a valid overall rating.

## Synthetic example

An engineering lead records:

```text
M = 8
Evidence: direct manager assigned broader scope and named the lead's specific
contribution in two cross-team reviews.

S = 5
Evidence: next-level leader recognizes the project but cannot yet distinguish
the lead's contribution from the team's aggregate work.

E = unknown
Evidence: the work has not required senior sponsorship.
```

The profile is `8·5·unknown`. The useful conclusion is not "promotion blocked."
It is that next-level evidence is weaker than direct-manager evidence, while E
is currently untested and may not yet be relevant.
