# Module 06: Momentum and Resistance

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module adds time to M/S/E without inventing score velocity or career
predictions.

Momentum is:

> sustained improvement in the quality, reach, or resilience of relevant
> advocacy evidence over time.

Resistance is:

> an observed act, decision, or repeated pattern that blocks, reverses, or
> discounts relevant evidence.

Resistance describes behavior or process. It does not establish motive.

## Position and movement

An M/S/E profile is a current evidence position.

Two people with the same profile may have different evidence histories:

- one may have gained recent, specific, repeated advocacy;
- one may be relying on old evidence that has not been tested recently;
- one may be rebuilding after a manager or role change;
- one may have meaningful counterevidence;
- one may simply lack enough observation to know.

Do not infer movement from position alone.

## Track each dimension separately

Use one movement state per dimension.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strengthening | Relevant evidence is becoming more direct, specific, repeated, recent, tested, or resilient |
| Holding | The pathway remains supported and no material change was expected |
| Stalled | A defined evidence goal was tested through a relevant opportunity, but the pathway did not change |
| Weakening | Recent evidence is less specific, less repeated, contradicted, withdrawn, or no longer current |
| Resetting | A context change altered who observes, decides, or advocates, so prior evidence must be re-evaluated |
| Mixed or unclear | Evidence moves in different directions or remains insufficient |

Example:

```text
M: strengthening, medium confidence
S: stalled, low confidence
E: unknown
```

Do not collapse the states into one arrow or overall momentum number.

## What counts as strengthening

A pathway may strengthen when:

- inference becomes reported or direct evidence;
- generic awareness becomes specific understanding;
- one act becomes a repeated pattern;
- old evidence becomes recent;
- advocacy is maintained under disagreement, scarcity, cost, or risk;
- evidence reaches the relevant decision while action is still possible;
- one brokered route becomes several legitimate, informed routes;
- contribution remains accurate through a transition.

A higher score is not itself proof of momentum. The score may have changed
because the assessment became more accurate.

## What does not establish momentum

- busier work;
- more meetings or introductions;
- praise without an advocacy act;
- one high-visibility event;
- a title change;
- a score increase without new evidence;
- repeated self-promotion;
- a favorable outcome with no known pathway;
- optimism about a future conversation.

Momentum concerns evidence movement, not activity volume or confidence.

## The movement record

```text
Dimension:
Prior evidence state:
New evidence or event:
What changed:
Counterevidence:
Context change:
Movement state:
Confidence:
Next evidence condition:
Review trigger:
```

Example:

```text
Dimension: S
Prior evidence state: next-level leader had generic awareness
New evidence or event: leader cited two specific contributions in a resource
decision and asked the contributor to join the follow-up
What changed: awareness became specific and created direct access
Counterevidence: no evidence yet of advocacy under scarcity
Context change: none
Movement state: strengthening
Confidence: medium
Next evidence condition: accurate representation in another consequential forum
Review trigger: next resource or scope decision
```

Use events or decisions as review triggers where possible. Do not rescore on a
fixed calendar merely to create motion.

## Holding is not stalled

A pathway is holding when:

- current evidence remains relevant;
- no meaningful opportunity to test a change occurred;
- no agreed change was expected;
- the context is stable enough that the evidence still applies.

A pathway is stalled only when:

1. a desired evidence change was defined;
2. a responsible actor and bounded action were identified;
3. a relevant opportunity to test the change occurred;
4. the expected observable change did not occur;
5. alternative explanations and counterevidence were considered.

No universal number of days or cycles proves a stall.

## The bounded evidence experiment

Use a finite test rather than indefinite effort.

```text
Pathway:
Desired evidence change:
Why it matters:
Bounded action:
Responsible owner:
Relevant opportunity:
Observable response:
Safety and workload limit:
Result:
Interpretation:
Next decision:
```

Possible results:

- movement occurred;
- no movement, with a plausible ordinary explanation;
- counterevidence changed the claim;
- the route is structurally unavailable;
- an observed decision blocked the route;
- the experiment became unsafe or too costly;
- evidence remains insufficient.

The experiment tests a pathway. It does not manipulate a person into providing
support.

## Friction, counterevidence, resistance, and uncertainty

These conditions require different responses.

### Friction

A known structural condition makes evidence movement slower, costlier, or less
available without evidence of active blocking.

Examples:

- inaccessible decision forums;
- unclear ownership;
- organizational change;
- workload that leaves no development capacity;
- location or schedule barriers;
- a process that records only final outcomes.

Response: redesign the route, clarify ownership, change the opportunity, or
assign organizational responsibility.

### Counterevidence

Relevant evidence weakens or challenges the claim being advocated.

Examples:

- a promised outcome did not occur;
- readiness evidence is incomplete;
- another person has stronger evidence for the opportunity;
- behavior created material harm.

Response: understand, correct, narrow, or retire the claim. Counterevidence is
not resistance merely because it is unfavorable.

### Resistance

An observed act, decision, or repeated pattern blocks, reverses, or discounts
relevant evidence.

Examples:

- agreed evidence criteria change after they are met;
- accurate credit is repeatedly removed;
- a supported opportunity is withdrawn without an applicable reason;
- relevant evidence is excluded while less relevant evidence is accepted;
- protected feedback triggers retaliation.

Response depends on rationale, recurrence, power, safety, and available review
routes.

### Uncertainty

Evidence is too sparse or ambiguous to classify the condition.

Response: preserve `unknown`, seek a bounded clarification if safe, and avoid
story-building.

## Resistance is behavior, not motive

Do not infer:

- a secret campaign;
- hidden coordination;
- jealousy;
- retention intent;
- coordinated opposition;
- discriminatory motive;

from vague answers, delay, one denied request, reduced warmth, or hearsay.

Record:

```text
Observed blocking act or decision:
Decision owner:
Stated rationale:
Relevant evidence considered:
Relevant evidence omitted:
Consistency with prior criteria:
Recurrence:
Affected pathway:
Safety or power concern:
Alternative explanations:
Confidence:
Available review or correction route:
```

Bias, discrimination, harassment, or retaliation may be present. When the
evidence indicates a serious concern, use an appropriate formal and independent
process rather than a career-scoring diagnosis.

## Resets

A reset can follow:

- a manager or sponsor change;
- a new role or profession;
- a transfer;
- a reorganization;
- extended leave;
- a major strategy or ownership change;
- a move between organizational contexts.

A reset does not mean all evidence becomes zero.

Classify prior evidence:

| Evidence | Treatment after reset |
|---|---|
| durable contribution record | carry forward with context |
| relationship-specific advocacy | re-evaluate |
| current-role readiness | test against the new role |
| old decision commitment | confirm with the current owner |
| direct working relationship | may continue if still relevant |
| title- or assignment-based exposure | do not treat as sponsorship |

During a reset, `unknown` may be more accurate than either stable or weakening.

## Weakening evidence

Weakening may include:

- a supporter withdraws or materially narrows a claim;
- recent direct evidence contradicts older reported evidence;
- contribution is no longer represented accurately;
- repeated decision opportunities pass without previously observed advocacy;
- a relationship or role change makes old evidence stale;
- a formal concern changes what can responsibly be advocated.

Do not treat ordinary disagreement, delayed response, or one quiet period as
inevitable decline.

## Stop conditions

More effort is not always the answer.

Pause or change route when:

- the experiment creates retaliation or safety risk;
- a legitimate decision-maker has given a clear final answer;
- the required opportunity does not exist;
- evidence needed for the claim is not present;
- repeated attempts impose disproportionate workload or emotional cost;
- criteria continue to change without a review path;
- a structural barrier belongs to the organization to repair;
- the desired outcome no longer fits the person's goals.

Possible next decisions:

- revise the claim;
- seek explicit criteria;
- use a formal correction or appeal path;
- build another legitimate evidence route;
- wait for a relevant opportunity;
- change role, team, or organization;
- stop pursuing the outcome.

Stopping one strategy is not proof of failure.

## Equity and access

Evidence movement is shaped by:

- proximity to decision-makers;
- work schedule and location;
- role visibility;
- employment arrangement;
- language and communication norms;
- disability and accessibility;
- sponsorship access;
- bias and discrimination.

Do not tell people to compensate for structural barriers with unlimited
visibility labor. Record the barrier and identify the owner able to change it.

## M/S/E interpretation

Movement remains evidence-based and dimension-specific.

```text
M strengthening:
recent direct-manager advocacy becomes more specific, repeated, or tested

S stalled:
a defined next-level evidence condition was tested but did not change

E resetting:
a sponsor or organizational context changed and prior evidence needs review
```

Do not:

- subtract ordinal scores as velocity;
- sum movement across dimensions;
- calculate momentum from M/S/E;
- convert resistance into a score;
- predict timing from the movement state;
- treat strengthening as guaranteed advancement;
- treat weakening as inevitable exit.

## Organization responsibilities

Organizations should:

- publish relevant decision criteria and owners;
- preserve evidence through role and manager changes;
- provide correction and appeal routes;
- identify structural access barriers;
- prevent retaliation for protected feedback or inquiry;
- avoid changing criteria after evidence is produced;
- distinguish a legitimate denial from unexplained obstruction;
- reduce dependence on informal proximity;
- communicate when a pathway or opportunity does not exist.

## What this module does not claim

- Momentum makes advancement inevitable.
- A score difference is a meaningful velocity.
- A fixed number of cycles proves a stall.
- Delay proves active opposition.
- Resistance is always malicious.
- Counterevidence is resistance.
- Distributed work carries a universal penalty.
- Reorganization resets all evidence to zero.
- More effort always overcomes a structural barrier.
- A movement state predicts promotion, retention, or exit.
