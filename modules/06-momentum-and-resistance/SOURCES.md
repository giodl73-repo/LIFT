# Module 06 Public Sources

These sources support adjacent concepts. They do not validate the LIFT movement
states, a career momentum formula, a resistance score, recovery curves, or
promotion predictions.

## Momentum and change

- Jansen, K. J. (2004), "From Persistence to Pursuit: A Longitudinal
  Examination of Momentum During the Early Stages of Strategic Change,"
  *Organization Science*, 15(3), 276-294.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1040.0064).

  This longitudinal organizational study treats momentum as dynamic and
  socially reinforced during strategic change. LIFT borrows the
  position-versus-movement distinction cautiously; organizational change
  momentum is not an individual career predictor.

## Resistance and organizational change

- Ford, J. D., Ford, L. W., and D'Amelio, A. (2008), "Resistance to Change:
  The Rest of the Story," *Academy of Management Review*, 33(2), 362-377.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2008.31193235).

  This article challenges treatment of resistance as inherently dysfunctional
  and examines how change agents and processes may contribute to it. LIFT uses
  that caution to avoid equating unfavorable behavior with malicious motive.

- Oreg, S., Vakola, M., and Armenakis, A. (2011), "Change Recipients'
  Reactions to Organizational Change: A 60-Year Review of Quantitative
  Studies," *Journal of Applied Behavioral Science*, 47(4), 461-524.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0021886310396550).

  This review presents reactions to change as cognitive, emotional, and
  behavioral responses shaped by personal and situational factors. It does not
  validate LIFT's use of resistance for advocacy pathways.

## Career shocks and transitions

- Akkermans, J., Collings, D. G., da Motta Veiga, S. P., Post, C., and
  Seibert, S. (2021), "Toward a Broader Understanding of Career Shocks:
  Exploring Interdisciplinary Connections With Research on Job Search, Human
  Resource Management, Entrepreneurship, and Diversity," *Journal of
  Vocational Behavior*, 126, 103563.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2021.103563).

  This review treats career shocks as contextual events that can alter career
  direction and adaptation. The field remains developing and does not provide
  a universal reset or recovery curve.

- Mussagulova, A., Chng, S., Goh, Z. A. G., Tang, C. J., and Jayasekara,
  D. N. (2023), "When Is a Career Transition Successful? A Systematic
  Literature Review and Outlook (1980-2022)," *Frontiers in Psychology*, 14,
  1141202.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1141202).

  This review finds fragmented definitions and measures of successful career
  transitions. LIFT therefore avoids treating one movement state or outcome as
  universal success.

## Feedback seeking

- Balaceanu, A., et al. (2021), "Feedback-Seeking Behavior in Organizations:
  A Meta-Analysis and Systematical Review of Longitudinal Studies," *The
  Spanish Journal of Psychology*.
  [Publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/spanish-journal-of-psychology/article/abs/feedbackseeking-behavior-in-organizations-a-metaanalysis-and-systematical-review-of-longitudinal-studies/6FFDC8E5984077B6AD8C5BF8292A0A63).

  This source supports feedback seeking as a contextual learning behavior. It
  does not imply that feedback is always safe, accurate, or actionable.

## Ordinal measurement

- Verhulst, B., and Neale, M. C. (2021), "Best Practices for Binary and
  Ordinal Data Analyses," *Behavior Genetics*, 51, 204-214.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10519-020-10031-x).

  This review explains methods appropriate for ordinal data. LIFT applies the
  basic measurement boundary: ordered categories do not automatically justify
  equal-interval subtraction, averages, or physical-style velocity formulas.
