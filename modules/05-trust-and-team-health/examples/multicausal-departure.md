# Synthetic Example: A Departure With Multiple Causes

This example is invented to demonstrate the method.

## Situation

One team member leaves after eighteen months.

Available evidence includes:

- dissatisfaction with recent workload;
- a desired specialty unavailable on the current team;
- higher compensation elsewhere;
- positive comments about manager support;
- frustration with an organization-wide return-to-office policy.

## Interpretation

The departure is meaningful. It does not point to one cause.

The organization should:

- preserve the person's stated reasons without forcing one narrative;
- inspect whether workload concerns recur for others;
- separate manager-controlled and organization-controlled conditions;
- review contradictory evidence;
- avoid using the exit as either proof of manager failure or proof that
  management played no role.

Patterns across time may change confidence. Privacy still applies.
