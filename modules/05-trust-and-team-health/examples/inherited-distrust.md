# Synthetic Example: Inherited Distrust and Bounded Commitments

This example is invented to demonstrate the method.

## Situation

A new manager inherits a team that has experienced repeated priority changes,
unexplained cancellations, and feedback requests that produced no visible
action.

Team members are cautious in meetings and decline an invitation to discuss
trust.

## Unsafe interpretations

```text
The team is resistant and must give the new manager a fair chance.
The silence proves that the prior manager caused harm.
A team-building session will reset the relationship.
```

None of these conclusions follows from the available evidence.

## Bounded response

The manager says:

> I know priorities have changed repeatedly and that some earlier feedback did
> not produce a visible response. I do not need anyone to disclose private
> experiences or tell me that they trust me. For the next work cycle, I will
> publish priority changes with the decision owner and tradeoff. Please correct
> the model if it misses an important constraint. We will review whether that
> commitment was useful at the end of the cycle.

## Three records

```text
Management act:
Published two priority changes with owners, rationale, and displaced work.

Team experience:
One person corrected a missing dependency; broader safety and trust remain
unknown.

Operating context:
One priority changed again because an external dependency failed.
```

## Interpretation

The manager created evidence of clearer follow-through and made correction
possible.

The record does not claim:

- that prior harm has been established;
- that the team now trusts the manager;
- that one practice caused the correction;
- that people owe the manager more disclosure.
