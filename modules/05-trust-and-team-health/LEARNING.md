# Module 05 Learning

## Source lesson

The private source correctly argued that senior advocacy alone cannot establish
management quality. People on a team directly observe clarity, safety,
fairness, development, workload, and accountability that may remain invisible
upward.

It weakened that insight by creating a second score, multiplying it into an
advocacy profile, and treating surveys, departures, silence, and promotion
outcomes as deterministic signals.

## Changes in the public rewrite

- the hidden second score became a separate team-experience record;
- management acts, team experience, and operating context became three distinct
  evidence lanes;
- team health became five lenses rather than one number;
- trust was separated from liking, agreement, retention, and praise;
- contradiction became protected inquiry rather than automatic override;
- surveys gained response, missingness, subgroup, timing, and privacy limits;
- attrition became multi-causal rather than a manager verdict;
- silence became ambiguous rather than diagnostic;
- next-level conversations gained purpose, confidentiality, and anti-retaliation
  rules;
- serious misconduct was routed outside ordinary development feedback;
- fairness to the manager was made compatible with source protection;
- inherited distrust became a contextual condition rather than a defect to
  erase or a verdict on the new manager;
- trust repair became bounded commitments and observable follow-through
  without coerced disclosure or guaranteed outcomes;
- M/S/E remained an advocacy profile and did not absorb team sentiment.

## Open learning questions

- Can practitioners maintain three records without excessive process?
- Are the five team-health lenses concrete enough?
- How should mixed subgroup experiences be represented?
- Can managers receive actionable themes without identifying sources?
- What is a safe feedback design for teams of two or three?
- How should organizations communicate action after confidential feedback?
- Does the reconciliation table reduce both dismissal and premature judgment?
- Can a new manager establish useful commitments without asking people to
  declare trust or identify confidential sources?
