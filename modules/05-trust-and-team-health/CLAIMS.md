# Module 05 Claim Ledger

| Claim | Label | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Team health is the ability to coordinate, speak up, learn, sustain work, and receive fair support | LIFT definition | retain |
| Management acts, team experience, and operating context should be recorded separately | LIFT practice | retain |
| Psychological safety relates to learning, voice, and performance | externally supported | retain with measurement limits |
| Employee silence can reflect fear, futility, low efficacy, or other causes | externally supported | retain as ambiguity |
| Trust is willingness to accept vulnerability, not liking or agreement | externally supported concept | retain |
| Voluntary turnover has multiple individual, job, manager, and organizational antecedents | externally supported | retain |
| Multisource feedback can support development, with modest and context-dependent effects | externally supported | retain with safeguards |
| Upward support and team experience should be reconciled through inquiry | LIFT practice | retain |
| Team experience should be converted into a second M score | private synthetic rule | reject |
| Team feedback should multiply an upward advocacy score | private synthetic formula | remove |
| Survey scores directly measure manager quality | unsupported simplification | reject |
| One departure is decisive negative evidence regardless of reason | unsupported simplification | reject |
| Team-health concerns automatically override all other evidence | unsupported universal rule | reject |
| Silence or praise reliably reveals team trust | unsupported inference | reject |

## Open evidence needs

- independent use of the three-record model;
- privacy-safe practices for teams too small for anonymous surveys;
- examples of fair reconciliation under conflicting evidence;
- evidence about subgroup experience hidden by team averages;
- methods for distinguishing development issues from formal misconduct;
- team-health practices in frontline, shift, distributed, and contingent work;
- evidence about feedback systems that produce action without retaliation;
- counterexamples where direct next-level access harms trust or coordination.
