# Module 07 Public Sources

These sources support adjacent concepts. They do not validate the LIFT six-lens
review, pre-join M/S/E inference, manager scoring, entry-path formulas, or
career predictions.

## Realistic job previews

- Phillips, J. M. (1998), "Effects of Realistic Job Previews on Multiple
  Organizational Outcomes: A Meta-Analysis," *Academy of Management Journal*,
  41(6), 673-690.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5465/256964).

  The meta-analysis reports modest, context-dependent relationships with
  expectations, recruitment attrition, performance, and turnover. A realistic
  preview does not eliminate uncertainty or guarantee fit.

## Applicant attraction and fit

- Chapman, D. S., Uggerslev, K. L., Carroll, S. A., Piasentin, K. A., and
  Jones, D. A. (2005), "Applicant Attraction to Organizations and Job Choice:
  A Meta-Analytic Review of the Correlates of Recruiting Outcomes,"
  *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 90(5), 928-944.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.90.5.928).

  This meta-analysis identifies relationships among organizational
  characteristics, recruiter behavior, perceived fit, attraction, and job
  choice. Attraction is not evidence that the environment will be healthy.

- Kristof-Brown, A. L., Zimmerman, R. D., and Johnson, E. C. (2005),
  "Consequences of Individuals' Fit at Work: A Meta-Analysis of Person-Job,
  Person-Organization, Person-Group, and Person-Supervisor Fit,"
  *Personnel Psychology*, 58(2), 281-342.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00672.x).

  The meta-analysis reports relationships between several forms of perceived
  fit and work attitudes or outcomes. Fit definitions and measurements vary,
  much evidence is correlational, and fit language can reinforce similarity
  bias if used carelessly.

## Interview structure

- Levashina, J., Hartwell, C. J., Morgeson, F. P., and Campion, M. A. (2014),
  "The Structured Employment Interview: Narrative and Quantitative Review of
  the Research Literature," *Personnel Psychology*, 67(1), 241-293.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12052).

  This review supports structured, consistent, job-relevant interviewing over
  unstructured impression gathering. LIFT applies the principle symmetrically:
  candidates also benefit from consistent environment evidence.

## Pre-join expectations

- Rousseau, D. M. (1989), "Psychological and Implied Contracts in
  Organizations," *Employee Responsibilities and Rights Journal*, 2(2),
  121-139.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01384942).

  This article describes individual beliefs about reciprocal obligations that
  can be shaped by recruitment messages and informal promises. It does not make
  every pre-join statement a legal commitment.

## Online inference and privacy

- Roth, P. L., Bobko, P., Van Iddekinge, C. H., and Thatcher, S. M. B. (2016),
  "Social Media in Employee-Selection-Related Decisions: A Research Agenda for
  Uncharted Territory," *Journal of Management*, 42(1), 269-298.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206313503018).

  This article identifies relevance, validity, privacy, protected-information,
  and applicant-reaction questions in social-media screening. LIFT uses those
  concerns to reject covert online profiling in either direction.
