# Module 07 Learning

## Source lesson

The private source correctly expanded offer evaluation beyond title and
compensation. It also recognized that candidates should inspect manager
practice, organizational access, stability, and equity.

It undermined those ideas by inferring hidden advocacy scores from career
history, assigning deterministic value to entry paths, profiling prospective
managers through former reports, and presenting generated hiring classifiers
as validated.

## Changes in the public rewrite

- manager scoring became reciprocal environment diligence;
- pre-join M/S/E became `unknown`;
- six environment lenses replaced inferred career trajectory;
- specific examples, written terms, and corroboration replaced profile
  classification;
- reverse references created reciprocal evidence access;
- entry paths became sources of context and expectation, not score assignments;
- criteria categories replaced a weighted offer total;
- a minimum-diligence route supported candidates with limited leverage;
- online profiling and covert employee contact were prohibited;
- fit gained a similarity-bias warning;
- employers gained an environment evidence brief;
- employer use of LIFT for candidate selection was explicitly prohibited;
- all hiring accuracy, entry score, timing, promotion, and compensation
  predictions were removed.

## Open learning questions

- Are six lenses usable during a real hiring process?
- Which questions produce specific rather than rehearsed answers?
- Will organizations provide reverse references?
- Can an environment evidence brief remain current?
- Does the minimum path help candidates with low leverage?
- How should conflicting but plausible accounts be represented?
- Can candidates distinguish realistic uncertainty from avoidable vagueness?
