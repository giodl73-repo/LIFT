# Module 04: New-Manager Transition

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module helps a person move from primarily individual contribution into
people management without erasing craft, overclaiming team results, or
retreating permanently into familiar execution.

The central evidence shift is:

```text
from: what I personally produced
to:   the conditions the team can understand, decide, learn, deliver, and sustain
```

This is a shift in emphasis, not a universal prohibition on direct work.

## What changes

Different roles combine individual and management work differently. The
transition still changes the questions used to inspect contribution.

| Individual-contribution emphasis | Management emphasis |
|---|---|
| What did I personally produce? | What conditions did I create or repair? |
| Was my decision correct? | Were decision rights and context clear? |
| Did I solve the hard problem? | Did the team gain capability and resilience? |
| Did I carry the work through? | Did I enable ownership without abandonment? |
| Can I explain the system? | Can the team understand and operate the system? |
| Did leaders see my contribution? | Did accurate team evidence travel? |

The right column does not make the manager solely responsible for every team
outcome. Staffing, strategy, resources, discrimination, inherited systems,
market conditions, and individual choices also matter.

## Four transition passes

The passes are repeatable. They are not a universal 30-, 60-, or 90-day clock.

```text
Learn the system
  -> establish contracts
  -> shift the work
  -> inspect and adapt
```

### Pass 1: Learn the system

Before redesigning the team, understand:

- the team's purpose and current commitments;
- each person's role, capability, goals, and constraints;
- customer and stakeholder expectations;
- decision history and unresolved disagreement;
- workload, operational risk, and inherited debt;
- existing strengths worth preserving;
- where context, evidence, resources, and opportunities travel;
- which problems the new manager has authority to change.

Listening does not require promising that every current practice will remain.
It prevents novelty from being mistaken for improvement.

Useful questions:

```text
What is working that I should not disrupt?
Where do decisions currently stall?
Which expectations conflict?
What do people avoid raising, and why?
Which commitments lack an owner or resource?
What management support has been missing?
```

Silence is not proof that the team feels safe or agrees.

### Pass 2: Establish contracts

Make the management relationship inspectable.

#### With the team

Clarify:

- what the manager owns;
- what individuals and groups own;
- which decisions are delegated;
- how disagreement and escalation work;
- how one-to-ones, feedback, and development conversations will be used;
- how credit and evidence will be represented;
- which existing practices will continue while learning occurs.

#### With the manager's manager

Clarify:

- expected management outcomes;
- retained individual-contribution duties;
- authority, budget, and staffing constraints;
- support, mentoring, and escalation paths;
- how inherited conditions will be separated from current management acts;
- which evidence will be used to understand progress.

A title without authority, support, or workload change is not a complete
management transition.

### Pass 3: Shift the work

Move effort toward observable management work.

#### Frame

- connect work to purpose and constraints;
- define outcomes without prescribing every action;
- make tradeoffs and uncertainty visible.

#### Enable

- provide resources, information, access, and clear decision rights;
- remove avoidable blockers;
- delegate ownership with appropriate support.

#### Observe

- inspect outcomes, interactions, and system behavior;
- seek disconfirming evidence;
- avoid substituting presence or activity for progress.

#### Develop

- provide task-relevant feedback;
- understand goals without promising unavailable outcomes;
- create fair learning and opportunity;
- recognize capability growth and support needs.

#### Represent

- attribute contribution accurately;
- carry evidence, risks, and resource needs through the organization;
- create direct and resilient pathways where appropriate.

This work may be less immediately visible than personal production. It still
requires evidence.

### Pass 4: Inspect and adapt

Review both management acts and the conditions around them.

```text
What management action occurred?
What changed, if anything?
What evidence supports the connection?
What counterevidence exists?
Which inherited or structural conditions mattered?
Who experienced the change differently?
What should continue, stop, or be tested next?
```

Do not claim that a positive team outcome proves excellent management or that a
negative outcome proves management failure.

## The output refuge

The output refuge is:

> repeated return to familiar personal execution because it is clearer, faster,
> more controllable, or more visibly rewarded than management work.

Possible signs:

- the manager repeatedly takes back work rather than repairing delegation;
- difficult one-to-ones become task-status meetings;
- decisions wait for the manager even when others have relevant capability;
- team results are narrated primarily through the manager's personal actions;
- feedback, development, hiring, or context work is continually postponed;
- the team cannot operate important routines without the manager present.

These signs do not diagnose motive, personality, or permanent unsuitability.
They identify a work-design question.

Ask:

```text
What management work is being displaced?
Why is personal execution currently the safer or more rewarded choice?
Is delegation missing context, capability, authority, or support?
Has the organization preserved an impossible individual workload?
What is the smallest ownership transfer that can be tested safely?
```

## When direct contribution is appropriate

A manager may contribute directly when:

- the role is explicitly player-coach;
- the team is small;
- an incident or temporary gap requires help;
- specialist judgment cannot yet be transferred;
- pairing or demonstration develops capability;
- the manager owns a bounded deliverable.

Guardrails:

1. make the reason and duration visible;
2. do not compete with the team for the most valuable work;
3. preserve the named owner and shared credit;
4. avoid becoming the permanent approval bottleneck;
5. plan the handoff or capability transfer;
6. check which management work is being deferred.

Direct work is a problem when it repeatedly substitutes for management rather
than supporting it.

## Delegation as a contract

Delegation is not task disposal.

```text
Outcome:
Named owner:
Decision rights:
Constraints:
Available context:
Support and check-in:
Escalation conditions:
Evidence of completion or learning:
```

The manager remains accountable for the quality of the delegation and the
conditions they control. The owner remains accountable for decisions within
the agreed scope.

Avoid:

- delegating responsibility without authority;
- retaining every meaningful decision;
- rescuing at the first sign of a different approach;
- using "autonomy" to abandon support;
- taking ownership back without explaining why;
- treating one mistake as proof that delegation failed.

## Feedback and development

Management evidence may include:

- expectations expressed before evaluation;
- timely, task-relevant feedback;
- specific recognition and accurate credit;
- recurring discussion of capability and goals;
- supported opportunities with clear rationale;
- changed support after learning what does not work.

Feedback volume is not the goal. Feedback can harm when it is vague,
self-focused, threatening, contradictory, or disconnected from a usable action.

Development is not a promise of promotion, title, or preferred work.

## Team experience without a hidden score

Team experience matters because people observe management directly.

Potential evidence includes:

- whether people can explain goals and decision rights;
- whether risks and disagreement can be raised;
- whether feedback and support are usable;
- whether workload and opportunity are distributed fairly;
- whether the team can operate without recurring rescue;
- whether contribution is represented accurately.

Do not:

- create a secret "downward M" score;
- treat engagement data as a promotion formula;
- infer trust from silence;
- treat every departure as proof against the manager;
- expose identifiable feedback without an agreed process;
- reward popularity over clarity, fairness, or accountability.

Module 05 will examine trust and team health in depth.

## The transition evidence record

```text
Management condition:
Observed management act:
Team or system signal:
Counterevidence:
Inherited or structural context:
Evidence date:
Confidence:
Next bounded experiment:
```

Example:

```text
Management condition: decision clarity
Observed management act: published ownership and escalation rules
Team or system signal: two decisions completed without manager approval
Counterevidence: incident decisions still route only through the manager
Inherited or structural context: on-call policy names the manager as approver
Evidence date: 2026-08-07
Confidence: medium
Next bounded experiment: delegate one incident class with explicit rollback
```

This record does not produce an automatic M/S/E score.

## M/S/E interpretation

A role change can create uncertainty because the evidence being observed has
changed.

```text
M: Does the direct manager understand and advocate for the person's actual
   management work?

S: Does the next level have accurate evidence about team conditions,
   management acts, constraints, and learning?

E: Does senior awareness, advocacy, or sponsorship exist based on relevant
   management evidence rather than title or team size alone?
```

Do not reset a score automatically on the transition date. Update the evidence
record as old evidence becomes less relevant and new evidence appears.

## Organization responsibilities

Organizations should not promote a person into management and leave the
transition entirely to individual improvisation.

Useful support includes:

- an explicit role and authority contract;
- reduced or bounded prior workload;
- practical manager education;
- an experienced peer or mentor;
- access to people, policy, and operational support;
- recurring feedback from the manager's own manager;
- safe routes to ask basic questions;
- evaluation that distinguishes inherited conditions from current choices;
- a respected path back to individual contribution.

The team should not bear the full cost of an unsupported manager transition.

## Choosing not to manage

People management is not a universal advancement requirement.

A person may reasonably conclude that they:

- prefer specialist or individual work;
- do not want authority over employment decisions;
- cannot accept the current workload or support model;
- want leadership without people management;
- tried management and want to return to another path.

Returning to individual contribution is not automatically failure. A healthy
organization makes the choice discussable without stigma.

## What this module does not claim

- A new manager's M score resets to a fixed number.
- A universal transition clock predicts success.
- Spending a fixed percentage of time on direct work proves failure.
- Prior craft expertise becomes irrelevant.
- Team trust begins at zero.
- Team outcomes are caused only by the manager.
- Delegation means maximal autonomy in every decision.
- Psychological safety means comfort, agreement, or lack of accountability.
- Management is the required next step for strong individual contributors.
- A manager title creates leadership identity or legitimacy automatically.
