# Synthetic Example: The Expert Rescuer

This example is invented to demonstrate the method.

## Situation

A new manager is the team's most experienced specialist. When delivery slows,
the manager takes over the hardest work, resolves it quickly, and receives
praise for saving the project.

The team increasingly waits for the manager. One-to-ones are postponed, and
two people stop volunteering for complex ownership.

## Diagnosis

```text
Useful direct contribution: the immediate problem was resolved
Management work displaced: delegation repair, capability development, feedback
Output refuge: recurring rescue is clearer and more rewarded than enablement
Structural context: leadership praises short-term rescue and preserved the
manager's prior workload
```

## Bounded change

For the next complex issue, the manager:

- names another owner;
- shares diagnostic context;
- agrees on check-in and escalation conditions;
- pairs only on the highest-risk step;
- lets the owner present the result;
- records which management duties now have protected time.

The goal is not for the manager to stop helping. It is to make help increase
team capability rather than dependency.
