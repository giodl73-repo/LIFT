# Module 08: Transitions

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module helps people preserve useful evidence and rebuild changed
relationships when a manager, role, team, organization, or period of work
changes.

The central rule is:

> a transition changes the applicability and ownership of evidence. It does
> not erase a person's history or create a justified score by formula.

Do not "reset the person." Remap the evidence.

## Scope

Module 04 addresses the shift from individual contribution into management.
This module addresses continuity when reporting, authority, role, team,
organization, or work availability changes.

## The transition loop

```text
Snapshot
  -> Map
  -> Carry
  -> Contract
  -> Observe
  -> Reassess
```

### 1. Snapshot

Preserve a dated record before the change when possible:

- role and scope;
- current M/S/E evidence and counterevidence;
- work outcomes and contribution attribution;
- active decisions and dependencies;
- development and opportunity commitments;
- material unknowns;
- evidence owners and dates.

The snapshot is history. It should not be silently overwritten by the new
state.

### 2. Map

Map the current decision structure:

```text
Who is the direct manager?
Who now acts as the next-level decision maker?
Who holds senior sponsorship or resource authority?
Who is interim?
Which decisions moved?
Which relationships remain relevant but no longer hold authority?
```

M, S, and E attach to current functions and authority, not permanent people or
titles.

### 3. Carry

Carry relevant evidence with its source and limits:

- completed work and observable outcomes;
- accurate contribution and shared credit;
- current responsibilities;
- documented feedback;
- active commitments;
- known risks and unresolved disagreements;
- context needed to interpret the record.

Carried evidence is not inherited advocacy. A new manager receiving a strong
handoff has context, not a preassigned M score.

### 4. Contract

Establish the new operating contract:

- role and decision rights;
- priorities and constraints;
- communication and feedback;
- attribution and representation;
- active commitments and their owners;
- transition support;
- review triggers;
- correction path for material mismatch.

Do not assume the old contract still applies.

### 5. Observe

Collect current behavior:

- Does the new manager provide context and resources?
- Is prior contribution attributed accurately?
- Are commitments acknowledged and dispositioned?
- Can the person access the forums their role requires?
- Are changed expectations made explicit?
- Does the organization respond when the handoff is wrong?

Current advocacy requires current evidence.

### 6. Reassess

Reassess when enough relevant behavior exists or when a named trigger occurs.

Do not manufacture a score to fill the period between change and evidence.
`Unknown`, `paused`, and ranges remain valid.

## Evidence status after a transition

| Status | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Continuing | same relevant actor or route retains authority and recent evidence still applies | a next-level leader remains in role and continues representing the work |
| Carried | prior evidence was transferred with provenance but the recipient has not acted on it | an incoming manager received the contribution record |
| Paused | the relationship or work remains relevant but there has been no fair opportunity for current evidence | a person is on leave or a decision process is frozen |
| Stale | evidence is historically valid but no longer sufficient for the changed scope or context | feedback from a substantially different role |
| Retired | the relationship or route no longer performs that M, S, or E function | the former manager left the organization |
| Unknown | current evidence is absent or cannot yet distinguish a judgment | a new manager has not made or represented a relevant decision |

Status belongs to the evidence path, not the person's worth or capability.

## Historical and current profiles

Keep both:

```text
Pre-transition profile:
Evidence date:
Context:

Current M:
Status:
Current evidence:

Current S:
Status:
Current evidence:

Current E:
Status:
Current evidence:
```

Example after a manager departure:

```text
Pre-transition M: 8-9, observed through named advocacy and opportunity backing
Current M: unknown
Status: prior M retired; work evidence carried to incoming manager
Current S: 6-7, continuing
Current E: unknown, no current evidence opportunity has occurred
```

Do not write:

```text
New manager baseline M = 5
```

No universal transition baseline exists.

## Work evidence is not advocacy evidence

A transition can preserve:

- what was delivered;
- who contributed;
- the difficulty and context;
- prior feedback;
- a decision already made.

It may not preserve:

- a former manager's current authority;
- a promised future action;
- the receiving manager's judgment;
- access to a forum that moved;
- a relationship tied to the prior role.

Preserve the first category. Re-establish or clarify the second.

## The transition record

```text
Transition:
Effective date:
Work-relevant change:

Pre-transition role and profile:
New role and authority map:

M evidence status:
S evidence status:
E evidence status:

Evidence carried:
Evidence not portable:
Commitments requiring disposition:
Privacy exclusions:
Material unknowns:

New operating contract:
Support owner:
Review triggers:
Correction path:
```

The person should be able to review material statements about their work where
law and safety permit.

## The handoff packet

A bounded handoff can include:

1. current role, scope, and decision rights;
2. observable contributions with accurate attribution;
3. active work, dependencies, and risks;
4. documented feedback and unresolved counterevidence;
5. development or opportunity commitments;
6. work requirements or arrangements the person has agreed may be shared;
7. what needs confirmation from the person;
8. record owner and date.

Do not include:

- speculation about personality, loyalty, ambition, or motives;
- private health, family, caregiving, or leave details;
- protected characteristics;
- rumors or unattributed claims;
- old ratings without their scope, date, and evidence;
- a prediction of success under the new manager;
- information collected through surveillance.

The handoff should preserve context without pre-judging the new relationship.

## The commitment ledger

Transitions often erase promises through silence.

Record material commitments:

```text
Commitment:
Original owner:
Authority to make it:
Date:
Conditions:
Evidence:
New owner:
Disposition:
  continuing | reconfirmed | revised | completed | closed | unknown
Reason:
Review date:
```

A prior statement is not automatically binding on a new decision maker. It is
also not acceptable to pretend it never happened.

Reconfirm, revise, complete, or close it explicitly.

## Manager change

When a manager changes:

- retire the former manager as the current M route;
- preserve the former manager's dated evidence;
- mark the new M as unknown until relevant behavior exists;
- map interim and permanent decision authority;
- carry work, attribution, commitments, and counterevidence;
- confirm which S and E routes actually continue;
- establish a new operating contract;
- provide a correction route for an incomplete or inaccurate handoff.

### Responsibilities of the departing manager

Where safe and possible:

- complete accurate work and contribution records;
- identify shared credit;
- disposition decisions they own;
- name unresolved risks and disagreements;
- document commitments and their authority;
- review the factual handoff with the person;
- avoid diagnosing future performance.

### Responsibilities of the incoming manager

- treat the handoff as evidence, not a verdict;
- ask what changed or may be stale;
- hear the person's account;
- clarify expectations and decision rights;
- identify which commitments they can honor;
- observe before assigning a precise M judgment;
- correct known attribution errors.

### Responsibilities of the organization

- name interim authority;
- preserve access to records and essential forums;
- prevent review deadlines from silently erasing evidence;
- provide continuity for every report, not only highly visible people;
- resolve conflicting commitments;
- avoid making employees compete for transition attention.

There is no universal 30-, 60-, or 90-day point at which the new manager's
view becomes fixed.

## Internal transfer

An internal transfer does not preserve every relationship just because the
employer name stays the same.

Map:

- the new manager;
- the actual next-level and senior decision routes;
- which former advocates retain authority;
- which work evidence remains relevant;
- which access depended on the prior role;
- what the receiving team needs to learn;
- what the person needs to unlearn or update.

The receiving manager's M begins as `unknown`, not a favorable default.

Prior S or E evidence can continue only when:

- the same actor still performs the relevant function;
- they retain authority or influence over the new work;
- current behavior supports the judgment.

Provide transfer onboarding. Organizational familiarity does not guarantee
role clarity, team integration, or access.

Do not require a person to disclose conflict, retaliation, health, or private
reasons to justify a transfer beyond what the formal process requires.

## Reorganization

A reorganization can change reporting lines, scope, layers, forums, and
decision rights without changing anyone's title.

Create an authority map:

```text
Decision:
Old owner:
New owner:
Effective date:
Pending cases:
Evidence location:
Escalation route:
```

Then review:

- which M/S/E functions moved;
- whether work attribution survives the move;
- whether active reviews or opportunities still have an owner;
- which commitments require reconfirmation;
- who communicates changed criteria;
- how people correct an inaccurate mapping.

Do not silently cancel commitments or guarantee that every old commitment must
continue. Disposition them.

Do not infer declining advocacy from a temporary absence of forums during the
reorganization.

## Leave and return

Leave can include parental, caregiving, medical, bereavement, public service,
sabbatical, or other approved absence. The reason may be private.

During leave:

- preserve the dated record;
- mark unavailable evidence opportunities as `paused`, not weakening;
- assign temporary work and decision ownership;
- limit contact to agreed needs;
- protect private information;
- do not require visibility or networking to preserve standing;
- do not interpret absence as reduced commitment or capability.

Before return, confirm:

- current role and reporting line;
- material changes in scope, priorities, systems, and team;
- agreed work requirements or accommodations through the appropriate process;
- access and training;
- decisions made during leave that affect the role;
- evaluation period and evidence expectations;
- a safe correction and support route.

On return:

- use reboarding rather than assuming immediate familiarity;
- restore access before judging execution;
- separate changed context from capability;
- evaluate work performed, not time absent;
- avoid forcing disclosure of the leave reason;
- record current evidence as it becomes available.

A previous M/S/E profile is historical evidence. It is not automatically
restored or penalized.

## External move

An external move normally changes all three current organizational routes.

Keep:

- dated work evidence;
- references and prior observed advocacy;
- portable professional relationships;
- public work and externally verifiable outcomes.

Mark current M/S/E as unknown until the new authority structure and behavior
are observable.

A referral or senior placement may provide context or access. It does not seed
automatic scores.

## Returning to a prior organization

Returning employees may possess useful organizational knowledge and
relationships. The organization, role, people, and authority may also have
changed.

Use:

- ordinary role diligence;
- a current authority map;
- explicit comparison of old and new conditions;
- reboarding;
- current evidence.

Do not assume:

- prior high performance will repeat unchanged;
- prior advocates retain authority;
- external reputation transfers into E;
- the original reason for leaving is resolved;
- the return path guarantees a premium or faster advancement.

Research on returning employees reports mixed and context-dependent outcomes,
not a universal advantage.

## Following a manager

Following a manager to another team or organization may preserve familiarity.
It does not preserve the surrounding system.

Inspect:

- the manager's actual authority in the destination;
- the new S and E routes;
- role scope and written terms;
- whether the manager can deliver the stated opportunity;
- dependency if the manager changes again;
- alternatives for evidence portability.

Prior M evidence remains relevant history, but current M still requires behavior
in the new context.

## Scope or role change

When responsibility changes materially:

- mark prior evidence with its old scope;
- identify what remains comparable;
- establish new success conditions;
- preserve shared credit;
- avoid treating an untested duty as failure;
- reassess only after a fair evidence opportunity.

A title change alone does not prove that advocacy, authority, or scope changed.

## Transition fairness

Transitions often disadvantage people with:

- less senior access;
- nonstandard schedules;
- leave or caregiving responsibilities;
- disability or accommodation needs;
- remote or distributed work;
- weaker informal networks;
- prior exclusion;
- contingent or contract status;
- lower bargaining power.

Organizations should:

- provide a role-consistent core continuity process with accessible
  adjustments;
- make handoff and authority information accessible;
- audit whose evidence or commitments were lost;
- avoid rewarding only people who self-promote during disruption;
- offer correction without retaliation;
- protect privacy;
- separate missing opportunity from poor performance.

## Stop conditions

Stop and route to an appropriate formal process when:

- the transition involves alleged misconduct, retaliation, discrimination, or
  harassment;
- health, disability, leave, immigration, or employment rights require
  specialist handling;
- the handoff contains contested serious allegations;
- decision authority is legally or contractually unclear;
- preserving a source would create a credible safety risk.

LIFT does not replace HR, legal, union, accessibility, security, or
investigative processes.

## Prohibited uses

Do not use this module to:

- assign automatic transition scores;
- predict promotion or failure by a fixed clock;
- preserve a rating without its evidence and scope;
- create a hidden succession or flight-risk list;
- penalize leave, transfer, or nonlinear careers;
- pressure a person to follow a manager;
- infer private reasons for change;
- share protected or irrelevant personal information;
- rank people by relationship portability;
- treat a missing handoff as evidence against the person.

## What this module does not claim

- Every transition resets M/S/E to a known value.
- Internal transfers preserve S and E automatically.
- A new manager's view is fixed after 90 days.
- Prior manager advocacy transfers to a new context.
- Senior placement bypasses current evidence.
- Leave weakens commitment or capability.
- A return employee has a universal advantage.
- One former blocker determines the next outcome.
- Reorganizations must preserve or cancel every old commitment.
- A clean handoff guarantees a successful transition.

Public sources support adjacent ideas about newcomer adjustment, manager
behavior, work-role transitions, internal mobility, returning employees, and
return-to-work conditions. They do not validate LIFT transition statuses,
automatic score resets, inherited M/S/E, recovery clocks, or career forecasts.
