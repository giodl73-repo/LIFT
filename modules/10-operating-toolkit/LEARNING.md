# Module 10 Learning

## Source lesson

The private source correctly used dated tracking, evidence rationale, history,
action planning, and reassessment.

It embedded those useful practices in a record centered on personal
identifiers, employer levels, score sums, pattern labels, gradients, promotion
readiness, 90-day targets, and stay-or-move recommendations. Related schemas
and dashboards expanded the record toward a centralized assessment system.

## Changes in the public rewrite

- one master tracker became seven optional small templates;
- module records remained authoritative;
- private profile and shared conversation were separated;
- filled records became private by default;
- score history required evidence and context;
- score totals, patterns, gradients, gates, and predictions were removed;
- action plans became one bounded pathway experiment;
- fixed cadence became event-based review;
- personal names and employer identifiers became unnecessary;
- ethical-use checks became mandatory before sharing or reuse;
- archive and deletion became operating steps;
- dashboards, databases, automation, and HR integrations became anti-features;
- software gained explicit admission and shutdown gates;
- the toolkit introduced no new empirical outcome claim.

## Open learning questions

- Are seven templates still too many?
- Can a first-time user choose the correct record?
- Does the private/shared split prevent oversharing?
- Which fields are essential after actual use?
- Do users understand review triggers without reminders?
- Can records be closed and deleted in practice?
- Does Markdown exclude people who need other accessible formats?
- What repeated burden, if any, could justify software?
