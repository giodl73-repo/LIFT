# Module 02 Public Sources

These sources support adjacent concepts. They do not validate the LIFT advocacy
sequence, a universal behavior ranking, a manager bandwidth threshold, or
promotion predictions.

## Mentorship, sponsorship, and opportunity

- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2019),
  *The Science of Effective Mentorship in STEMM*.
  [Full report](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25568/the-science-of-effective-mentorship-in-science-technology-engineering-mathematics-and-medicine).

  The report treats advocacy, access, and sponsorship as important elements of
  developmental relationships and gives particular attention to inclusion.
  Its setting is STEMM education and careers, not every workplace.

- Ibarra, H. (2022), "What We Still Misunderstand About Mentorship and
  Sponsorship," *Harvard Business Review IdeaCast*.
  [Transcript](https://hbr.org/podcast/2022/11/what-we-still-misunderstand-about-mentorship-and-sponsorship).

  This source distinguishes advice and development from sponsorship that uses
  organizational and political capital. It also discusses similarity bias and
  difficulties in formal sponsorship programs.

## Credit attribution

- Heilman, M. E., and Haynes, M. C. (2005), "No Credit Where Credit Is Due:
  Attributional Rationalization of Women's Success in Male-Female Teams,"
  *Journal of Applied Psychology*.
  [DOI record](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0021-9010.90.5.905).

  The study demonstrates attribution bias under ambiguous contribution in a
  specific experimental context. LIFT uses it to justify careful attribution,
  not to predict every team or identity pattern.

## Opportunity allocation

- Gilmartin, S. K., et al. (2023), "Early-Career Assignments and Workforce
  Inequality in Engineering," *Engineering Studies*.
  [Article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19378629.2023.2272807).

  This research examines how early-career assignments can shape unequal
  workforce outcomes in engineering. LIFT applies the lesson as a reason to
  inspect opportunity distribution while preserving domain and population
  limits.

## Feedback and documented evidence

- Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development, "Performance feedback: an
  evidence review."
  [Evidence review](https://www.cipd.org/globalassets/media/knowledge/knowledge-hub/evidence-reviews/performance-feedback-evidence-review_tcm18-111378.pdf).

  This review informs the distinction between timely feedback and durable,
  actionable evidence. It does not rank formal documentation against informal
  sponsorship.
