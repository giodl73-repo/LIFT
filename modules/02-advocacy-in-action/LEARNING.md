# Module 02 Learning

## Source lesson

The private source usefully named several concrete behaviors: context relay,
specific attribution, opportunity creation, introductions, and advocacy in
another person's absence.

It weakened those ideas by presenting generated rankings and a fixed advocacy
capacity as empirical law. It also framed concentration on a few
promotion-ready reports as efficient rather than examining fairness,
development, consent, and system design.

## Changes in the public rewrite

- ranked behaviors became the See-Name-Connect-Open-Back-Transfer sequence;
- "name-saying" became accurate, contextual credit attribution;
- the fixed three-person limit became a bounded sponsorship portfolio with no
  universal threshold;
- an advocacy floor was added for every team member;
- sponsorship selection gained explicit criteria and distribution review;
- stretch work gained consent, support, and workload checks;
- formal and informal advocacy became complementary;
- direct access became a way to reduce dependence on one manager;
- advocacy after failure gained an honesty contract;
- equity and attribution-bias evidence became first-class;
- promotion probabilities and score effects were removed.

## Open learning questions

- Is the six-action sequence memorable and distinct?
- Does an advocacy floor change manager behavior?
- Can managers review distribution without creating bureaucracy?
- How should sponsorship capacity be discussed for very large teams?
- When does direct access become bypassing or organizational noise?
- How can managers honor consent when opportunities move quickly?
- Does the module help less-visible contributors receive accurate credit?
