# Module 09: Equity and Ethical Use

Status: draft

## Purpose

This module defines when LIFT evidence is safe enough to use, when unequal
access changes its interpretation, and when a record must be restricted,
discarded, or routed outside the framework.

The central rule is:

> safety before score.

A score may still describe an absent advocacy path under unequal opportunity.
It is not comparable evidence of capability, effort, or potential. Evidence
collected unsafely or used for an unauthorized purpose must be restricted,
discarded, or routed.

Read the repository-wide [Ethical Use Policy](../../ETHICAL_USE.md).

## LIFT is not an employment-decision system

LIFT may support:

- private self-reflection;
- a disclosed development conversation;
- a manager examining their own advocacy acts;
- a privacy-bounded review of organizational pathways;
- synthetic learning.

LIFT scores must not decide:

- who is hired;
- who is promoted or paid more;
- a performance rating;
- discipline or termination;
- layoffs or succession;
- who receives immigration, leave, accommodation, health, or benefit support;
- who is labeled loyal, productive, high potential, risky, or likely to leave.

The framework has not been validated for those uses. Disclosure or human review
does not make an invalid use valid.

## Observation is not capability

M/S/E describes observable advocacy evidence in a current context.

A low or unknown score does not establish:

- weak performance;
- low potential;
- poor judgment;
- lack of ambition;
- weak commitment;
- bad relationships;
- personal failure;
- future outcome.

Example:

```text
Observed S: 1-2
Evidence: the next-level leader has not received specific work evidence
Structural context: the role is excluded from the review forum
Interpretation: next-level advocacy is absent
Not established: contribution quality, readiness, or effort
```

Do not add points to "correct" inequity. Record the unequal pathway and change
the process that created it.

## Ethical lifecycle

```text
Purpose
  -> Limit
  -> Inspect
  -> Protect
  -> Contest
  -> Expire
```

### 1. Purpose

State:

- why the record exists;
- who benefits;
- what decision it may and may not inform;
- who owns the process;
- what would make the purpose end.

Do not collect a score "in case it becomes useful."

### 2. Limit

Collect the minimum:

- observable advocacy behavior;
- source and date;
- relevant context;
- counterevidence;
- uncertainty;
- the person's response where appropriate.

Do not collect private motives, protected characteristics, relationship gossip,
or broad digital exhaust.

### 3. Inspect

Inspect whether:

- the evidence opportunity existed;
- criteria were known;
- the source was legitimate;
- access was distributed fairly;
- the same behavior was interpreted consistently;
- missing evidence reflects missing opportunity;
- a safer source or method exists.

### 4. Protect

Define:

- who can see the record;
- where it is stored;
- whether it can be copied or exported;
- small-group and source-identification risks;
- how confidential material is separated;
- what happens after an access or privacy failure.

### 5. Contest

Provide:

- the material claim;
- its source type and date;
- a way to add context and counterevidence;
- a correction process;
- contested status when disagreement remains;
- a protected route for safety or retaliation concerns.

Procedural fairness does not require exposing a confidential source when that
would create harm.

### 6. Expire

Set:

- a review date;
- a purpose-ending condition;
- deletion or archival responsibility;
- exceptions required by applicable law or formal policy.

Do not keep individual scores indefinitely because storage is cheap.

## The ethical-use gate

Before using a LIFT record, answer:

| Question | If no |
|---|---|
| Is the purpose permitted and disclosed? | do not use |
| Is each material claim observable and relevant? | remove or mark unknown |
| Did a fair evidence opportunity exist? | record the access gap; limit interpretation |
| Are protected and private data excluded? | separate or delete; route if required |
| Can the person review or safely contest material claims? | restrict use |
| Is retaliation risk acceptably controlled? | stop and use a protected route |
| Are access and retention bounded? | do not share or centralize |
| Is a human accountable for the use? | stop automation |
| Would the record affect an employment decision? | do not use LIFT |

Possible dispositions:

```text
use for permitted reflection
restrict to a narrower audience or purpose
discard unsupported or excessive data
route to a qualified formal process
```

## Opportunity before interpretation

Review the path that could have produced evidence:

| Stage | Question |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Who knew the opportunity, criteria, or forum existed? |
| Eligibility | Who was treated as eligible, and by what job-relevant rule? |
| Nomination | Who could nominate themselves or others? |
| Selection | Who decided, using which evidence and criteria? |
| Support | Who received time, context, resources, coaching, or cover? |
| Exposure | Whose work reached M, S, or E through legitimate routes? |
| Attribution | Whose contribution was named accurately? |
| Evaluation | Was similar evidence interpreted consistently? |
| Remedy | Could omissions or errors be corrected safely? |

Review repeated distributions, not one isolated outcome.

A low score can accurately describe absent advocacy while still reflecting an
unfair system. The score does not explain the cause.

## Access conditions to record

Relevant conditions can include:

- role and work type;
- schedule and time zone;
- remote, hybrid, field, shift, or customer-site work;
- language and communication format;
- accessibility and agreed work requirements;
- manager span and organizational layers;
- forum design;
- project assignment;
- resource and travel availability;
- contract or employment structure;
- prior exclusion or retaliation risk.

Record work-relevant conditions, not private reasons or identity inference.

## Protected and private information

An individual LIFT record must not include or infer:

- race, ethnicity, nationality, caste, or religion;
- sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, pregnancy, or family status;
- age;
- disability, diagnosis, genetic, or health information;
- leave reason;
- immigration or citizenship detail beyond an authorized work requirement;
- union or protected activity;
- political belief;
- private relationship, financial, or family information;
- any other characteristic protected in the relevant jurisdiction.

This list is not a legal definition. Requirements vary by place and context.

It can be legitimate to record a work requirement:

```text
Needs written material in an accessible format.
Available during the agreed schedule.
Role requires authorized travel.
```

Do not record why unless a qualified process requires it.

## Separate the development and equity-audit lanes

### Development lane

Contains:

- an identifiable person's observable evidence;
- current work context;
- counterevidence;
- agreed development questions.

It excludes protected characteristics and is not used for employment
decisions.

### Equity-audit lane

A qualified organizational process may examine aggregate distributions using
separately governed demographic data when authorized and appropriate.

It should define:

- the precise disparity question;
- the data owner and authority;
- collection and access controls;
- privacy and re-identification review;
- group and intersection limits;
- statistical and practical uncertainty;
- retention and deletion;
- who can act on the result;
- how affected people learn what changed.

Do not copy protected data back into individual LIFT profiles.

Do not infer identity from names, images, schools, locations, language, social
media, or career history.

## Aggregate pathway review

A privacy-bounded pathway review may count:

```text
eligible people
people informed
people considered
people selected
people who declined
people who received support
people whose contribution was attributed
people whose omission was corrected
```

It may review criteria and rationale.

It must not:

- publish identifiable individual scores;
- rank managers by team M/S/E averages;
- assume a small group is anonymous because names were removed;
- treat demographic disparity as proof of individual intent;
- use an aggregate pattern to change an individual's score;
- expose a complainant or confidential source.

There is no universal safe minimum group size. If people can reasonably be
identified from context, suppress or redesign the report.

## Bias review

Ask:

- Are criteria job-relevant and defined before seeing the person?
- Is the same behavior described differently across people?
- Do ambiguous words such as "polish," "fit," "presence," "style," or
  "executive" hide an unstated norm?
- Are group contributions attributed consistently?
- Are availability and visibility being mistaken for impact?
- Are career gaps, lateral moves, remote work, accent, or communication format
  used as proxies?
- Does one informal network control nomination and context?
- Who can safely disagree with the judgment?

Replace ambiguous labels with observable behavior and context.

Do not create a demographic correction formula.

## Consent under power

In a workplace, a request from a manager or employer may feel mandatory.

Consent is not enough when:

- refusal can affect work, opportunity, or reputation;
- the purpose is unnecessary or prohibited;
- excessive data is collected;
- the person cannot withdraw or correct;
- the audience or secondary uses are unclear;
- the safety risk is material.

For optional participation:

- explain the purpose and audience;
- provide a genuine alternative;
- impose no adverse consequence for declining;
- allow questions and correction;
- collect no more than necessary;
- confirm whether withdrawal and deletion are possible.

Do not require employees to disclose a self-score to prove self-awareness.

## Anti-retaliation

No person should face adverse treatment for:

- declining optional LIFT participation;
- recording `unknown`;
- challenging a score or source;
- adding counterevidence;
- identifying unequal access;
- refusing unsafe visibility or networking labor;
- using a protected concern route;
- requesting privacy, accessibility, or correction.

When direct inquiry is unsafe:

- do not require confrontation;
- preserve uncertainty;
- use a trusted or qualified channel;
- separate development from investigation;
- minimize identifying detail;
- stop the LIFT process if it increases risk.

Potential discrimination, harassment, retaliation, threats, fraud, or serious
misconduct belongs in an appropriate independent process.

## Privacy and data governance

Every shared record should name:

```text
Record subject:
Purpose:
Owner:
Source types:
Authorized audience:
Storage location:
Copy or export rule:
Review date:
Deletion or archive condition:
Correction route:
Protected concern route:
```

Defaults:

- keep private self-reflection under the individual's control;
- do not centralize records without a demonstrated permitted need;
- store the summary rather than unnecessary raw material;
- restrict access by role and purpose;
- do not reuse for a new purpose without a new review;
- remove access when the purpose ends;
- delete when retention is no longer justified;
- follow applicable law, agreements, and formal policy.

## AI and automated tools

Do not submit identifiable personnel data, confidential employer records, or
private feedback to a public AI service.

Even in an approved environment, do not use AI to:

- infer M/S/E from email, chat, calendars, recordings, cameras, badges, social
  networks, or activity logs;
- infer protected or private characteristics;
- decide hiring, promotion, pay, performance, discipline, termination, layoff,
  succession, or retention risk;
- identify likely complainants or dissenters;
- manufacture missing evidence;
- present generated judgment as observed fact.

Permitted assistance may include:

- formatting a record the person already controls;
- checking whether evidence fields are complete;
- suggesting neutral questions;
- summarizing deidentified or synthetic material;
- identifying unsupported certainty for human review.

Generated content must remain labeled, reviewable, and attributable to a human
owner.

## Monitoring and digital exhaust

Do not infer advocacy or commitment from:

- message volume or response time;
- meeting count or camera use;
- office attendance or badge data;
- calendar proximity to leaders;
- social-network centrality;
- document edits or keystrokes;
- sentiment, facial, voice, or emotion analysis;
- public or private social-media activity.

These signals are context-poor, easy to game, privacy-invasive, and likely to
reflect role, schedule, disability, caregiving, location, or access.

Use direct work evidence and disclosed sources.

## Goodhart protections

Do not:

- set score targets or quotas;
- reward managers for team score averages;
- optimize mentions, meetings, introductions, or executive contact counts;
- sum M/S/E into a talent rank;
- treat score movement as a promotion forecast;
- compare people whose opportunity contexts differ;
- require continuous visibility labor;
- hide structural barriers by increasing a score.

Improve the behavior and pathway, not the metric.

## Responsibilities

### Individual

- controls private self-reflection;
- chooses whether to share in optional development use;
- can add context, counterevidence, and contested status;
- is not responsible for repairing structural inequity alone.

### Manager

- examines their own observable advocacy acts;
- makes criteria and opportunity allocation inspectable;
- does not demand private scores;
- provides correction and no-retaliation routes;
- protects reports from unnecessary data collection.

### Senior leader

- owns pathway design and distribution;
- prevents one manager or network from controlling all access;
- funds privacy, accessibility, and protected channels;
- acts on aggregate process findings without targeting individuals.

### People, legal, privacy, security, accessibility, or union functions

Where applicable:

- own formal processes within their authority;
- govern protected and demographic data;
- define retention, access, and investigation boundaries;
- keep LIFT out of decisions it is not validated to make.

## Misuse response

If LIFT is being used outside its permitted purpose:

1. stop collection and automated processing;
2. prevent further sharing;
3. preserve only what a qualified process requires;
4. notify the appropriate owner where safe;
5. correct or delete unsupported data;
6. document the prohibited use and remediation;
7. assess retaliation and source-protection risk;
8. reopen affected module guidance if the misuse was foreseeable.

Do not use the LIFT process itself to investigate serious wrongdoing.

## What this module does not claim

- Equal scores mean equal opportunity.
- A demographic correction makes an individual score fair.
- Protected identity can be inferred safely.
- Consent makes every workplace data use ethical.
- Removing names guarantees anonymity.
- More monitoring creates better evidence.
- An AI model can infer advocacy from digital exhaust.
- A human reviewer makes a prohibited decision use acceptable.
- Silence means agreement or safety.
- Aggregate disparity proves individual bias or intent.
- LIFT is validated for employment decisions.

returns, network structure, psychological safety, employee voice, workplace
monitoring, privacy governance, algorithmic control, and hiring-system claims.
They do not validate LIFT scores, demographic correction formulas, or any
employment-decision use.
