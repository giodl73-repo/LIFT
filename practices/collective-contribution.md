# Represent Collective Contribution

Use this practice when an outcome depends on several kinds of work and a simple
hero story would erase contribution, context, or accountability.

## Central rule

Collective contribution does not mean everyone contributed equally.

It means:

> represent the distinct, evidence-supported work that made an outcome,
> decision, learning, or recovery possible.

Do not force a shared outcome into one person's narrative. Do not dissolve
individual accountability into "the team."

## Contribution functions

These functions are prompts, not titles or score categories.

| Function | Examples |
|---|---|
| Direction and decision | framed the problem, chose a tradeoff, accepted accountable risk |
| Creation and execution | designed, built, operated, delivered, or communicated the work |
| Enablement | supplied context, access, tooling, resources, coaching, or specialist help |
| Coordination | connected owners, resolved dependencies, sequenced work, maintained handoffs |
| Assurance | reviewed quality, safety, accessibility, evidence, or compliance |
| Maintenance and recovery | sustained the system, prevented recurrence, restored service, preserved continuity |

One person may perform several functions. A function may be absent or unknown.
Do not assign percentages or convert the map into a ranking.

## Build the contribution record

```text
Outcome, decision, or learning:
Evidence window:

Direction and decision:
Creation and execution:
Enablement:
Coordination:
Assurance:
Maintenance and recovery:

Shared dependencies:
Material disagreement or counterevidence:
Work that remains unverified:
Attribution that needs correction:
Who should explain which decision:
Consent or privacy limit:
Review and deletion event:
```

Record only functions relevant to the purpose. Empty fields should be removed,
not filled for completeness.

## Preserve both contribution and accountability

Ask:

- Who made which decision?
- Who performed which work?
- Who supplied conditions without which the work would have changed?
- Who accepted risk or owned a failure?
- Who corrected, maintained, or recovered the result?
- Which contribution is direct evidence, reported evidence, or inference?
- Which outcome had important external or inherited causes?

Shared credit does not require shared blame.

An accountable owner may own the decision while several people own distinct
contributions. A failure may involve individual action, system design,
resource constraints, and inherited conditions at the same time.

## Move collective evidence

Use the advocacy sequence:

```text
See the system of contribution
  -> Name distinct work accurately
  -> Connect it to the relevant decision
  -> Open direct explanation where appropriate
  -> Back accurate shared credit under pressure
  -> Transfer the pathway beyond one narrator
```

Possible actions:

- correct a summary that credits only the presenter;
- attach decision and maintenance owners to a launch record;
- invite the relevant contributor to explain their work, with consent;
- name enabling and assurance work in the forum where it mattered;
- preserve collaborative evidence through a manager or role transition;
- explain why an outcome cannot be attributed confidently.

## Common failures

### Hero compression

One visible person becomes the entire explanation.

Correction: name the work system and the person's actual role without
diminishing their contribution.

### Everyone did everything

Generic team praise removes meaningful attribution.

Correction: identify distinct functions and evidence without ranking human
worth.

### Outcome ownership inflation

A leader claims the result because they owned the organization or presented the
work.

Correction: separate formal accountability, decision ownership, execution, and
advocacy.

### Invisible-work romanticism

Hidden labor is praised but remains unsupported, overloaded, or structurally
invisible.

Correction: change workload, resources, access, ownership, or evidence paths;
do not substitute gratitude for repair.

### Percentage attribution

Contribution is divided into unsupported numerical shares.

Correction: use specific functions, acts, sources, and uncertainty.

## Synthetic example

A service improvement is presented by one project lead.

The evidence shows:

- an operator identified the recurring failure;
- an engineer implemented the change;
- a support specialist supplied customer examples;
- an accessibility reviewer changed the interaction;
- a release coordinator resolved two dependencies;
- the project lead made the release tradeoff and presented the result.

Accurate advocacy can name the project lead's real leadership while preserving
the other contributions.

It should not claim that every contribution had equal impact or that the list
is complete.

## What this practice does not claim

- Every contributor must be named publicly.
- More names always produce fairer attribution.
- Contribution can be measured by a universal taxonomy.
- Shared work eliminates individual accountability.
- Maintenance or enabling work should receive automatic employment outcomes.
- A contribution map should become a permanent personnel record.
