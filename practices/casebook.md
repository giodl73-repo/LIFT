# Integrated LIFT Casebook

This casebook shows how cross-cutting practices fit the LIFT journey.

Every scenario is synthetic. It demonstrates method, not a typical outcome or
validated intervention.

## Case 01: The work everyone relied on but nobody named

### Situation

A cross-functional service launch is presented successfully by a project lead.

The launch also depended on:

- an operator who identified the recurring failure;
- an engineer who implemented the change;
- a support specialist who supplied customer evidence;
- an accessibility reviewer who changed the interaction;
- a release coordinator who resolved dependencies.

The senior summary names only the project lead.

The release coordinator is asked to write separate weekly narratives and
collect endorsements so their contribution becomes visible.

## Observe

Separate observations from inference.

### Direct evidence

- incident records show the operator identified the recurring failure;
- change history identifies the implementation owner;
- review notes contain the accessibility correction;
- dependency records show the release coordinator's decisions;
- the senior summary credits only the project lead.

### Reported evidence

- the support specialist reports that customer examples changed priority;
- the manager reports that senior leaders saw only the final presentation.

### Inference to reject

```text
Senior leaders intentionally ignored the other contributors.
The project lead deliberately took all credit.
The release coordinator lacks influence because their work matters less.
```

Intent and relative value are not established.

## Assess

Question:

> Does the current advocacy path represent the release coordinator's actual
> contribution?

Provisional profile:

```text
M: 3-4, low confidence
Evidence: manager understands part of the coordination work but has not
represented it in the consequential forum

S: unknown
Evidence: no responsible evidence that the next level understands the work

E: unknown
Evidence: no relevant senior sponsorship evidence
```

This profile describes advocacy evidence, not contribution quality.

## Map

```text
Channel: evidence and credit
Needed movement: contribution evidence -> senior launch review
Formal route: contributor -> manager -> project lead -> review
Observed route: final presenter -> review
Failure: concentration and distortion
Who lacks necessary access: enabling, assurance, and coordination contributors
Fallback: source-backed shared contribution summary plus direct explanation
```

The process also creates evidence burden:

```text
Requested burden: separate weekly narratives and colleague endorsements
Who benefits: the existing review process
Who bears cost and exposure: the release coordinator
Smaller alternative: manager-owned source-backed contribution record
```

## Act

Choose bounded actions:

1. The manager builds one contribution record from existing work artifacts.
2. The project lead reviews and corrects the description of decision ownership.
3. Contributors review material statements about their own work.
4. The senior summary is amended rather than silently replaced.
5. The next review includes the relevant contributor when useful and wanted.
6. Weekly self-marketing narratives and political endorsements are not
   required.
7. The record receives a review and deletion event tied to the end of its
   purpose.

Collective contribution record:

```text
Direction and decision: project lead selected the release tradeoff
Creation and execution: engineer implemented the change
Enablement: support specialist supplied customer evidence
Coordination: release coordinator resolved dependencies
Assurance: accessibility reviewer changed the interaction
Maintenance and recovery: operator identified the recurring failure
Unknown: relative effect of each contribution on the final outcome
```

Repair record:

```text
Original claim: project lead delivered the service improvement
Problem: material shared contribution omitted
Disposition: add context and correct attribution
Audience: launch review recipients
Remaining uncertainty: relative contribution impact
```

## Reassess

After a consequential review:

```text
M: 5-6, medium confidence
Evidence: manager corrected the shared record and represented the coordinator's
specific work in the relevant forum

S: 5-6, low confidence
Evidence: next-level leader accurately described the coordination contribution
and included the owner in one follow-up decision

E: unknown
Evidence: no relevant senior advocacy opportunity occurred
```

The range changes because new advocacy evidence exists, not because the story
became more polished.

## What changed

- evidence burden moved from the contributor to the management pathway;
- shared contribution replaced hero compression;
- the original record remained traceable and was corrected;
- direct access reduced dependence on one presenter;
- unknown senior sponsorship remained unknown.

## What remains unresolved

- whether the new pathway will persist;
- whether less visible work is represented consistently across the team;
- whether the correction changed any downstream decision;
- whether the contribution record is small enough for routine use;
- whether the practice transfers to shift, frontline, or contingent work.

## Stop conditions

Stop or redesign if:

- the record becomes a permanent personnel profile;
- contributors are pressured to accept a public narrative;
- the process asks for protected or private information;
- correction increases retaliation risk;
- contribution functions become scores or percentages;
- managers use the record for employment decisions;
- the documentation cost exceeds the legitimate learning or pathway purpose.
