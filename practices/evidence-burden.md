# Reduce Evidence Burden

Use this practice when a person must repeatedly document, retell, expose, or
market legitimate work because ordinary management and organizational pathways
do not preserve it.

## Definition

Evidence burden is:

> the time, repetition, exposure, and risk required to make legitimate work
> evidence usable when ordinary pathways do not carry it reliably.

`Advocacy tax` can be a useful informal phrase. It is not a score or a claim
that every act of documentation is unfair.

Some documentation is part of the work. The concern is preventable,
duplicative, inaccessible, unsafe, or uncompensated evidence labor.

## Central rule

The person who did the work should not become the permanent archive, narrator,
and distribution system for that work.

Managers and organizations share responsibility for:

- understanding contribution;
- preserving source and context;
- attributing work accurately;
- carrying evidence into relevant decisions;
- maintaining correction paths;
- transferring access;
- retiring unnecessary records.

## Recognize the burden

Possible signs:

- the same contribution must be rewritten for several audiences;
- work is visible only when the person continually promotes it;
- enabling or maintenance work has no ordinary evidence path;
- remote, shift, field, or customer-site work requires extra proof;
- a transition repeatedly discards context;
- a person must collect endorsements to correct routine attribution;
- optional visibility work becomes an unspoken requirement;
- the evidence request exposes private information or retaliation risk;
- documentation labor is added without time, accessibility, or role support.

One request does not establish a pattern. Inspect recurrence, purpose, cost,
access, and alternatives.

## Allocate responsibility

| Actor | Reasonable responsibility | Should not be required |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | review material facts, add context or counterevidence, state preferences, preserve personally useful records | monitor private rooms, collect political endorsements, disclose protected information, maintain a shadow career database |
| Manager | observe work, preserve shared credit, maintain commitments, represent evidence, correct material errors | delegate all attribution and advocacy labor back to the person |
| Next-level leader | create direct evidence opportunities, inspect pathway failures, require fair attribution | rely only on polished self-narratives or manager reputation |
| Organization | design usable records and forums, support accessibility, preserve continuity, provide correction and protected routes | make self-marketing the default evidence system |

This allocation is a LIFT ethical principle, not a universal job description.

## Run the burden check

Before asking for new evidence, ask:

```text
Purpose:
Decision or conversation served:
Evidence already available:
Why existing evidence is insufficient:
Requested new work:
Who benefits:
Who bears the time, exposure, and risk:
Could the manager or process produce it instead:
Accessibility or workload support:
Audience and retention:
Correction and deletion event:
```

Do not request the record when:

- the purpose is vague or prohibited;
- ordinary work artifacts already answer the question;
- the request primarily compensates for manager inattention;
- a less exposed person must do more work only because they lack access;
- the record would become a performance, promotion, hiring, or talent profile;
- the person cannot safely refuse, correct, or limit it;
- there is no owner for use and deletion.

## Reduce the burden

Use the smallest applicable move:

1. **Reuse** an authorized work artifact instead of requesting a new narrative.
2. **Name** the contribution and shared credit at the point of work.
3. **Assign** a manager or process owner for evidence movement.
4. **Transfer** legitimate access so one person is not the permanent relay.
5. **Correct** the record once rather than requiring repeated rebuttal.
6. **Automate only structure**, not inference, using approved and purpose-limited
   tools.
7. **Expire** the record when the decision, learning purpose, or transition ends.

## Minimal burden record

```text
Evidence purpose:
Existing source:
Pathway failure:
Additional burden requested:
Burden owner:
Smaller alternative:
Safety or accessibility limit:
Correction route:
Stop and deletion event:
```

This record belongs to the process problem. It is not another employee profile.

## Examples

### Repeated launch narratives

A contributor is asked to rewrite the same launch story for a manager, review
forum, award nomination, and senior update.

Safer response:

- preserve one source-backed contribution record;
- tailor only the audience context;
- make the manager responsible for accurate relay;
- let the contributor review material attribution;
- delete nomination-only material when that purpose ends.

### Invisible maintenance work

Operational maintenance prevents incidents but produces no dramatic launch
artifact.

Safer response:

- use work records, prevented-risk evidence where supportable, reliability
  decisions, and maintenance ownership;
- do not require a crisis or heroic rescue before the work becomes legible;
- avoid inventing counterfactual impact that cannot be established.

### Unsafe self-advocacy

A person is told to obtain endorsements from colleagues after challenging an
attribution error.

Safer response:

- preserve the disputed claim and source;
- use the ordinary correction owner;
- do not require political campaigning;
- use a protected route when retaliation risk is material.

## What this practice does not claim

- All documentation is harmful.
- The person who did the work has no responsibility to provide facts.
- Managers can observe every contribution directly.
- One artifact is sufficient for every audience.
- Less documentation always creates more fairness.
- AI summarization is safe merely because it saves time.
