# Repair and Contest a Record

Use this practice when a material work claim, attribution, context statement,
commitment, or LIFT record may be wrong, incomplete, stale, or unsupported.

## Central rule

Correction should preserve what was known, what changed, and who decided.

Do not silently overwrite history. Do not preserve a known error merely because
it already influenced a conversation.

## Repair loop

```text
Notice
  -> Preserve
  -> Contest
  -> Decide
  -> Repair
  -> Close
```

### Notice

Name the material issue:

- factual error;
- attribution error;
- missing context;
- unsupported inference;
- stale scope or authority;
- commitment without an owner;
- decision recorded differently from what occurred;
- source or privacy problem.

### Preserve

Keep the prior statement, source type, date, audience, and known use when safe
and permitted.

Preservation creates traceability. It does not require retaining excessive,
private, harmful, or unlawfully held material.

### Contest

Provide:

- the claim being challenged;
- the person's correction or counterevidence;
- a safe response route;
- contested status while material disagreement remains;
- a protected route when retaliation or serious harm is possible.

Do not require the person to confront an unsafe actor or reveal a confidential
source.

### Decide

An accountable owner chooses a disposition:

```text
correct
add context
mark contested
retire as stale
restrict
delete
route to a qualified process
no change, with rationale
```

The owner should not be the subject of a serious allegation when independence
is required.

### Repair

Repair may include:

- amending a shared record;
- correcting attribution in the relevant forum;
- notifying an audience that relied on the error;
- dispositioning a broken commitment;
- restoring access or opportunity where possible;
- changing a pathway, criterion, or process;
- acknowledging uncertainty or unresolved disagreement;
- deleting unsupported or excessive data.

An apology without a changed record, owner, or pathway may be incomplete.

### Close

Record:

- the disposition;
- who was notified;
- what remains contested;
- whether downstream decisions require review;
- the next review or deletion event.

Close the repair record when no legitimate purpose remains.

## Minimal repair record

```text
Material claim or commitment:
Original source and date:
Observed problem:
Counterevidence or correction:
Safety, privacy, or retaliation limit:
Accountable disposition owner:
Disposition:
Repair action:
Audience requiring correction:
Remaining uncertainty:
Review and deletion event:
```

## Repair is not adjudication

LIFT can support correction of ordinary work records and advocacy pathways.

Potential discrimination, harassment, retaliation, fraud, threats, safety
issues, formal employment disputes, and legal rights require an appropriate
qualified or independent process.

Do not use a LIFT repair record to investigate witnesses, decide credibility,
or replace required process.

## Common failures

- editing history until the original error disappears;
- adding a note without correcting the audience that relied on the error;
- requiring repeated proof after a correction has been accepted;
- exposing a source to make the record easier to resolve;
- treating disagreement as disloyalty;
- preserving a contested score as settled fact;
- closing a commitment without naming who had authority to do so;
- using correction as an employment concession or bargaining tool.

## What this practice does not claim

- Every disagreement has one provable answer.
- Contestability requires publishing confidential sources.
- A corrected record reverses every downstream effect.
- An apology proves repair.
- LIFT can determine legal rights or misconduct.
- All prior records should be retained indefinitely.
