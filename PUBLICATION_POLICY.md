# Publication Policy

LIFT is public. Private workplace evidence may shape a question, but it does
not become public provenance.

## Do not publish

- employer, customer, product, project, team, or private program names;
- internal role levels, calibration rules, reward processes, or promotion
  documents;
- private people, conversations, files, links, prompts, assessments, or
  identifiers;
- exact confidential examples, scores, dates, compensation, populations, or
  outcomes;
- distinctive combinations of facts that identify a source;
- claims of employer endorsement or adoption.

## Public-safe material

Use:

- generic organizational layers and roles;
- synthetic or transformed examples;
- rounded or invented scores;
- observable management behaviors;
- independently accessible public sources;
- explicit evidence labels and limitations.

Changing only a company or product name is not sanitization. Rewrite the
mechanism, examples, numbers, timing, and surrounding facts.

## Re-identification review

Before publication, ask:

- Could a colleague identify the original person, team, or decision?
- Does the text preserve an internal level, process, title, or phrase?
- Are exact numbers necessary to teach the lesson?
- Is a synthetic or practitioner claim mislabeled as empirical?
- Could the advice encourage retaliation, favoritism, surveillance, or
  discriminatory inference?

If uncertain, transform further or do not publish.
