# LIFT

**A practical framework for understanding how advocacy, evidence, and
opportunity travel through an organization.**

Good work does not automatically become shared organizational evidence.

Managers, next-level leaders, and senior sponsors often see different parts of
a person's contribution. Context can arrive late. Credit can lose its owner.
Opportunity can depend on informal access. A manager can be supportive but lack
the authority or pathway needed to act.

LIFT makes those conditions easier to inspect without treating organizational
standing as human worth or predicting a career outcome.

## The core model

```text
M = direct-manager advocacy
S = next-level or skip advocacy
E = executive or senior-sponsor advocacy
```

Together, `M·S·E` describes the current evidence that relevant organizational
layers understand and support a person's work, scope, development, or
decisions.

Each dimension uses an ordinal 1-10 evidence range. Use `unknown` when the
evidence is not available.

Example:

```text
7 · 5-6 · unknown
```

This could mean:

- direct-manager advocacy has recent observable evidence;
- next-level understanding is present but inconsistent or untested;
- senior sponsorship cannot currently be assessed.

The weakest known dimension guides the next question. It does not declare
destiny.

Do not sum or average M/S/E.

## The LIFT journey

```text
Observe
  -> Assess
  -> Map
  -> Act
  -> Reassess
```

### Observe

Start with observable evidence, counterevidence, context, and unknowns.

Separate what you saw from what another person reported and what you inferred.

### Assess

Use an M/S/E range only after recording the evidence.

A score is a dated judgment about an advocacy path, not a rating of
performance, potential, loyalty, or worth.

### Map

Inspect how these channels travel:

- context;
- evidence;
- resources;
- opportunity;
- relationships.

A weak path may reflect absence, delay, distortion, concentration, dependency,
unequal access, changing authority, or simply missing evidence.

### Act

Choose one bounded action:

- ask one safer question;
- correct one attribution;
- clarify one commitment;
- open one fair evidence opportunity;
- repair one pathway;
- route a serious concern appropriately;
- wait for a relevant event.

The action is not "raise the score."

### Reassess

Reassess when evidence, scope, management, authority, safety, or organizational
conditions change.

Preserve uncertainty and prior context rather than rewriting history.

## Who LIFT is for

### Individuals

Use LIFT for private reflection or a disclosed development conversation.

It can help distinguish reassurance from evidence, identify an unknown path,
prepare a question, or preserve context through a transition.

### People managers

Use LIFT to inspect your own observable advocacy practice:

- accurate credit;
- useful context;
- fair opportunity;
- resource support;
- safe delegation;
- durable evidence pathways.

Do not secretly score reports.

### Leaders and organizational practitioners

Use LIFT to inspect whether pathways distribute context, evidence, resources,
and opportunity fairly.

Review processes and access conditions, not hidden employee profiles.

## Safety before score

LIFT is not a validated employment-decision system.

Do not use LIFT scores or inferred profiles for:

- hiring or candidate selection;
- promotion, pay, or performance ratings;
- discipline, termination, or layoff;
- succession, talent ranking, or workforce planning;
- loyalty, productivity, misconduct, or retention-risk scoring;
- covert monitoring or relationship mapping;
- automated or AI-generated employment recommendations.

Do not store LIFT scores in a shadow talent system.

Read the complete [Ethical Use Policy](ETHICAL_USE.md) before sharing,
aggregating, or reusing a record.

```text

  -> classify claims
  -> rewrite from first principles
  -> test with synthetic examples
  -> review with 17 adversarial roles
  -> record learning
  -> seek independent-human review
```
