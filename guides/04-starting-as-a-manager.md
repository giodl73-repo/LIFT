# Starting as a Manager

Use this guide when your evidence must shift from personal output toward the
conditions through which a team works.

Prior individual contribution remains part of your history. It is not complete
evidence of current management practice.

## Use four repeatable passes

```text
Learn the system
  -> Establish contracts
  -> Shift the work
  -> Inspect and adapt
```

These are repeatable passes, not a universal calendar.

## 1. Learn the system

Inspect:

- team purpose and current commitments;
- decision rights;
- inherited constraints;
- stakeholder and escalation paths;
- access to context, resources, and opportunity;
- existing safety or trust concerns.

Do not begin by diagnosing people from reputation or inherited labels.

### If distrust predates you

Do not ask the team for a clean slate or a declaration of trust.

Instead:

1. acknowledge known conditions and what remains unknown;
2. ask how those conditions affect current work, without soliciting private
   stories;
3. identify one commitment within your authority;
4. invite the team to correct the model or propose a safer alternative without
   requiring participation;
5. name the owner, limit, and review event;
6. follow through or explain promptly what changed.

When disagreement begins at the implementation detail, move outward:

```text
shared purpose
  -> observed work condition
  -> decision criteria and constraints
  -> smallest safe change
  -> implementation
```

This creates inspectable evidence. It does not prove that trust has been
repaired.

Use an appropriate formal or protected route for serious misconduct or harm.
Do not turn it into a trust-building exercise.

## 2. Establish contracts

Clarify:

- what the team owns;
- what you own;
- what each person can decide;
- how priorities change;
- how disagreement works;
- how credit is attributed;
- how support and correction are requested;
- what requires a formal route.

## 3. Shift the work

Management evidence may include:

- clearer goals and decisions;
- safe concern raising;
- appropriate delegation;
- fair opportunity;
- accurate attribution;
- useful context;
- resource and dependency removal;
- growth support aligned with a person's goals.

Avoid proving value by repeatedly taking back the most important work.

Personal rescue output can hide unclear ownership and prevent evidence of team
capability from forming.

## 4. Inspect and adapt

Keep three records separate:

1. management acts;
2. team experience;
3. operating context and outcomes.

One management act does not prove a team experience.

One person's experience does not describe the whole team.

An outcome does not reveal its cause by itself.

## A small first review

Choose one question:

```text
Can each person explain the decision they own?
Can someone raise a concern without losing access or opportunity?
Is contribution attributed to the people who did the work?
Does a dependency have a responsible owner?
Did I delegate authority or only tasks?
```

Record one observed act, one counterexample, one context condition, and one
review trigger.

## Common traps

- continuing to optimize primarily for personal output;
- using team sentiment as a manager score;
- demanding trust disclosure;
- treating inherited caution as resistance or disloyalty;
- using a trust exercise instead of repairing a work condition;
- treating silence as safety;
- giving opportunity only to the most visible people;
- rescuing work without clarifying ownership;
- using a fixed transition clock;
- confusing activity with management evidence.

Use the [team signal](../toolkit/templates/team-signal.md) only for a legitimate
management-learning purpose.

## What this guide cannot tell you

- whether you are promotion-ready;
- whether team members privately trust you;
- whether one practice caused an outcome;
- what every team or role should delegate;
- whether a serious concern belongs in coaching rather than a formal process.
