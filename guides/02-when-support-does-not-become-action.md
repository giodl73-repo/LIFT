# When Support Does Not Become Action

Use this guide when someone expresses support but a decision, resource,
opportunity, or commitment repeatedly remains unresolved.

Supportive words and organizational action are different evidence.

## Separate four questions

```text
Support:
  What has the person said directly?

Evidence:
  What specific advocacy act occurred?

Authority:
  What decision can this person make?
  What requires another owner?

Commitment:
  What action was agreed, by whom, under what conditions?
```

Do not begin by deciding that the person is dishonest, weak, blocked, or
unwilling. Those are possible explanations, not observed facts.

## Inspect the pathway

Map the relevant channel:

```text
Current owner:
Next owner:
Evidence that must travel:
Decision or resource:
Observed handoff:
Failure or uncertainty:
```

Possible pathway failures include:

- absence: no route exists;
- delay: the route exists but evidence arrives too late;
- distortion: the evidence changes as it travels;
- concentration: one relationship controls the route;
- dependency: action stops when one person is unavailable.

## Replace a promise with a bounded commitment

Ask:

> What is the next action you can own, what condition does it depend on, and
> what event should trigger our review?

Record:

```text
Commitment:
Owner:
Authority:
Conditions:
Evidence required:
Review trigger:
Correction route:
Disposition:
```

Do not require a guaranteed outcome. A responsible owner can commit to an
advocacy act, evidence opportunity, decision, or explanation.

They cannot guarantee a decision outside their authority.

## Review the event, not the optimism

At the review trigger, ask:

- Did the agreed act occur?
- What new evidence exists?
- What counterevidence appeared?
- Did authority or context change?
- Is the commitment complete, revised, transferred, or closed?

Avoid fixed clocks chosen only to create urgency.

## Options after review

Depending on the evidence, you may:

- continue the same route;
- clarify missing evidence;
- transfer the commitment to the actual owner;
- choose a different pathway;
- revise the goal;
- seek a protected or qualified route;
- close the question.

The guide does not choose among these for you.

## Common traps

- treating kind words as completed advocacy;
- asking for visibility rather than a relevant evidence opportunity;
- bypassing a manager without considering power and safety;
- repeating work for an already convinced audience;
- setting a score target instead of a decision or evidence condition;
- treating delay as proof of intent;
- waiting indefinitely because no review trigger was named.

Use the [pathway experiment](../toolkit/templates/pathway-experiment.md) and,
for a disclosed discussion, the
[shared conversation](../toolkit/templates/shared-conversation.md).

## What this guide cannot tell you

- why another person did not act;
- whether an employment outcome is deserved or likely;
- whether waiting or leaving is correct;
- whether escalation is safe;
- whether an unresolved commitment is discrimination, retaliation, ordinary
  delay, or another condition.
