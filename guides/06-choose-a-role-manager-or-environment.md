# Choose a Role, Manager, or Environment

Use reciprocal diligence to inspect the environment without scoring a
prospective manager, candidate, or organization.

Pre-join M/S/E remains `unknown`.

## Six lenses

### Role reality

- What work will I actually own?
- Which decisions come with the role?
- What changed since the role was opened?
- What would make the role unsuccessful even with strong work?

### Manager practice

- How are priorities and tradeoffs communicated?
- What happens when someone disagrees?
- How is contribution attributed?
- How are growth interests discussed?
- What decision can this manager make directly?

### Team conditions

- How is work distributed?
- How are concerns raised?
- What dependencies repeatedly slow the team?
- Whose perspective may be absent from the process?

### Evidence pathways

- How does work reach the people who allocate context, resources, and
  opportunity?
- Who can correct an inaccurate narrative?
- Is access dependent on one relationship?

### Opportunity

- Which opportunities are available?
- How are people selected?
- What support accompanies stretch work?
- Can someone decline without penalty?

### Stability and terms

- Which scope, reporting, location, or compensation terms are written?
- What remains subject to change?
- What happens if the manager or role changes?
- Which unknowns must be resolved before deciding?

## Compare sources, not people

Use:

- written terms;
- specific examples;
- multiple voluntary conversations;
- questions about observable practice;
- your own decision criteria.

Treat selected interview participants and polished stories as incomplete
evidence.

Do not:

- infer a manager's score;
- inspect former reports online to classify the manager;
- infer private team health;
- profile candidates or employees;
- assume senior involvement means sponsorship;
- treat a referral as guaranteed support.

## Write decision criteria

```text
Must be true:
Must not be true:
Strong preference:
Can learn after joining:
Unknown I can tolerate:
Unknown I cannot tolerate:
Written condition required:
Decision trigger:
```

The same evidence can lead different people to different decisions because
needs, constraints, risk, and power differ.

## Common traps

- choosing a role from title alone;
- choosing a manager from warmth alone;
- treating employee tenure as proof of health;
- interpreting one former report's outcome as manager quality;
- assuming candidate and employer have equal power;
- accepting changing scope without updating written terms;
- converting uncertainty into a manager score.

Use the
[environment-diligence record](../toolkit/templates/environment-diligence.md).

## What this guide cannot tell you

- whether the manager is good or bad;
- whether you will succeed;
- whether an interview answer predicts future behavior;
- whether another person's experience will match yours;
- whether accepting or declining is correct.
