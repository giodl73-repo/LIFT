# When Something Feels Wrong

Use this guide when working conditions, feedback, access, or relationships have
changed and you do not yet understand why.

Ambiguity is not proof of a hidden process.

It is also not a reason to ignore direct evidence or a safety concern.

## Record facts without building a signal score

```text
Observed change:
Date or event:
Direct evidence:
Counterevidence:
Ordinary explanations:
Serious explanations:
What I do not know:
Who has legitimate authority:
Safety or retaliation concern:
```

Examples of observable changes:

- responsibilities changed;
- a decision right moved;
- written feedback became more specific;
- a recurring meeting changed;
- a commitment was withdrawn;
- access to context or resources changed;
- expectations conflict.

None of these alone proves intent, misconduct, or an employment decision.

## Ask the smallest safe question

When direct inquiry is safe:

> I noticed this specific change. What changed in the work, expectations, or
> decision process, and what should I understand or do next?

Ask for:

- the current expectation;
- observable examples;
- the decision owner;
- available correction;
- the next review event;
- written clarification when appropriate.

Do not accuse another person based on an inferred pattern.

## Preserve your own evidence

Keep accurate records of:

- work-relevant decisions;
- expectations communicated to you;
- your responses and completed actions;
- factual corrections;
- commitments and owners.

Do not:

- secretly record where prohibited;
- collect colleagues' private information;
- build a relationship map;
- infer who discussed you;
- publish a personnel record;
- submit confidential material to a public AI service.

## Distinguish development from formal concerns

Ordinary development questions may stay in a disclosed work conversation.

Use a qualified formal or protected route when the facts may involve:

- discrimination;
- harassment;
- retaliation;
- safety;
- misconduct;
- a formal performance or employment process;
- accommodation, leave, health, immigration, or protected activity;
- legal rights or deadlines.

Do not use LIFT to investigate or adjudicate those concerns.

If there is immediate danger, use the appropriate emergency or safety path.

## Protect agency

You may:

- pause a direct conversation;
- ask for written expectations;
- bring an authorized support person where permitted;
- seek independent qualified advice;
- keep private reflection separate from shared records;
- decline to disclose a LIFT score;
- stop using LIFT when it increases risk.

## Common traps

- counting ambiguous events into a threat score;
- treating formality as proof of a predetermined outcome;
- assuming silence means safety;
- escalating through informal relationships without considering retaliation;
- using peers as covert sources;
- confronting someone with an inferred hidden narrative;
- waiting for certainty when a formal deadline or immediate safety issue exists;
- treating serious concerns as a coaching experiment.

Use the [ethical-use check](../toolkit/templates/ethical-use-check.md) before
sharing any record.

## What this guide cannot tell you

- whether you are being managed out;
- another person's motive;
- whether a formal process has begun;
- the legal meaning of an event;
- whether direct confrontation is safe;
- whether staying or leaving is correct.
