# When Access Is Uneven

Use this guide when opportunity, context, visibility, or advocacy appears to
depend on location, schedule, network, communication style, disability,
caregiving, role design, or another access condition.

Do not convert an access condition into a score penalty.

## Inspect the pathway

Choose one channel:

- context;
- evidence;
- resources;
- opportunity;
- relationships.

Record:

```text
Needed item:
Current route:
Who can use the route:
Who may be excluded:
Access prerequisite:
Whether the prerequisite is necessary:
Alternative route:
Owner:
Review trigger:
```

Focus on the process. Do not infer protected characteristics or put them in an
individual LIFT profile.

## Look for structural patterns

Questions:

- Does useful context travel only through optional live meetings?
- Does opportunity depend on informal proximity?
- Are important artifacts accessible after the event?
- Are selection criteria visible?
- Can people express interest privately and safely?
- Does the route require travel, schedule flexibility, or social access not
  required by the work?
- Is contribution attributed when the person is absent?
- Is there a correction route?

## Design one alternative

Examples:

- publish a decision note;
- rotate meeting times;
- make opportunity criteria visible;
- allow asynchronous evidence;
- identify contribution in the artifact;
- provide an opt-in route;
- offer an accessible equivalent format;
- transfer a relationship rather than requiring informal networking.

The alternative should preserve the legitimate purpose without demanding
unnecessary personal disclosure.

## Review without ranking people

Review:

- whether the route became usable;
- who still lacks legitimate access;
- new privacy or workload costs;
- counterevidence;
- whether the alternative should continue, change, or stop.

Do not:

- average employee LIFT scores;
- compare demographic groups through individual profiles;
- apply score corrections;
- infer effort or potential from access;
- require people to disclose why they need an alternative.

Aggregate equity auditing, when authorized, belongs in a separately governed
process with privacy and small-group protections.

## Common traps

- calling an opportunity open when information is uneven;
- treating attendance as contribution;
- making remote or asynchronous workers compensate with extra visibility
  labor;
- collecting protected information in the development record;
- assuming one alternative works for everyone;
- creating a shadow database to prove inequity;
- blaming individuals for a missing pathway.

Use the [pathway experiment](../toolkit/templates/pathway-experiment.md) for one
bounded change.

## What this guide cannot tell you

- whether discrimination occurred;
- which protected groups are affected;
- whether an accommodation is required;
- whether a score should be adjusted;
- whether one pathway change creates equity.
